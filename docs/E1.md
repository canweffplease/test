# E1: uncontended per-tier cost table

E1 answers one question for each congestion-control policy: how far above the
theoretical floor does a single KV-cache transfer land when nothing else is on
the network, as a function of how many switch tiers it crosses and whether it
is one flow or a TP=8 shard set. Every later experiment should be read against
these numbers.

Each result below has a mechanism behind it, and each mechanism is tied to
simulator code (`file:line`) and to a measurement from the logs. Nothing here
rests on what a protocol "should" do.

This version uses the **final, paper-faithful transports** and five ECMP seeds
per cell. The checkpoint 1 table (the old `build/` binaries, default seed only,
`-linkspeed 400000` on 100 G trees) and the analysis that went with it are kept
in Appendix A, marked legacy.

**Topologies.**

misconfiguration (400000 on 100 G trees, Appendix A.3) is gone.

**Seeds.** Seeds 1-5 for every policy and cell. The seed fixes the ECMP hash
salts, so the same seed is the same placement for RoCE, DCQCN and HPCC

All four policies are deterministic per seed: HPCC's wall-clock reseed is gone.

**DCQCN minimum-rate clamp.** Every DCQCN cell (table, K sweep, legacy row)

seed 3, is unchanged). The pre-clamp runs and summaries are in
`results/e1/dcqcn_preclamp/`.

**Metric.** Slowest-shard completion (the max over the run's flows) divided by
`167,772,160 B * 8 / C`:

- C = 400 Gb/s gives a floor of 3,355.4 us.
- C = 100 Gb/s (ft128) gives a floor of 13,421.8 us.

**Validity.** 329 logs in `results/e1/` (table, sweep, legacy row and
controls), **0** with "LOSSLESS not working". Every EQDS run has RTS = 0 and
0 stale NACKs. `results/e1/summary_seeds.csv` has a `violations` column, 0
everywhere.

**Continuity check.** RoCE at seed 13 on `build_start` with `-q 2000`
reproduces the legacy `build/` `-q 500` table to 0.1 us in all 13 cells, and
RoCE seeds 1-5 reproduce the old §4.3 seed study. So the RoCE column and the
ECMP placements did not move; only the transports and the seeds changed.

**Three caveats before anyone reads a column as a ranking:**

1. **The TP=8 rows are an ECMP lottery for RoCE, DCQCN and HPCC.** Their
   ranges span one or two collision classes (§4). Compare policies per seed,
   using the per-seed table above, never one seed against another.
2. **EQDS is not a congestion-control-only comparison.** It sprays each
   packet over 16 entropies; the other three pin each flow to one ECMP path.
   Most of EQDS's lead on the TP=8 rows is load balancing (§6.1).
3. **Buffers are matched in size, not in PFC thresholds.** RoCE and DCQCN
   pause at 150 packets of 4 KB per ingress; HPCC keeps its binary default
   of 15/12 packets of 9 KB (`datacenter/main_hpcc.cpp:67`, run.sh passes no
   `-pfc_thresholds` for HPCC); EQDS trims instead of pausing.

**How the table changed from checkpoint 1.** On the default TP=8 cell, RoCE
went from 3.05 (seed 13) to 2.04 (median of seeds 1-5): seed 13 is a 3-way
draw, the median placement is 2-way (§4.3). DCQCN went from 3.61 (legacy,
seed 13) to 3.44 (faithful + RED, median), but at matched seeds it is slower:
legacy DCQCN's median over seeds 1-5 is 2.45 (last row). The ft128 EQDS
singles went from 1.31-1.33 to 1.031 (the `-linkspeed` fix), HPCC now has
ft128 cells, and EQDS completes on 4os and 8os.

## 3. Single flows: where the 1.6-5.9% goes

### 3.1 RoCE and DCQCN

The result has an exact closed form, and the observed numbers match it to
0.1 us.

A RoCE source paces at the line rate from the first packet: `_pacing_rate = rate`
(`roce.cpp:78`). There is no window and no ramp. Each packet occupies
`(MSS + ACKSIZE)` on the wire:

- the spacing is set at `roce.h:48`
- the packet size is `size + ACKSIZE` at `rocepacket.h:28,46`
- ACKSIZE is 64 at `rocepacket.h:68`, and MSS is 4000 at `datacenter/main_roce.cpp:58`

The flow is done when the cumulative ACK covers the flow. So:

    T = ceil(S / 4000) * 4064 B * 8 / C          serialisation, 41,944 packets
      + h * 1 us                                 first packet's propagation over h links
      + (h - 1) * 4064 B * 8 / C                 store-and-forward at each switch
      + h * 1 us                                 the last ACK's return

At 400 Gb/s the serialisation term alone is 3,409.21 us. Adding the path terms:

| path | h | predicted | observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| same ToR | 2 | 3,413.29 | 3,413.3 |
| same pod | 4 | 3,417.46 | 3,417.5 |
| cross pod | 6 | 3,421.62 | 3,421.6 |

At 100 Gb/s the three paths give 13,641.2, 13,645.8 and 13,650.5 us, again
exactly as observed.

The whole gap to the floor therefore breaks down as follows:

- **Headers: 1.60%.** This is 64/4000. It accounts for nearly all of the gap.
- **Packet rounding: 0.002%.** 41,944 × 4000 B is 3,840 B more than the flow.
- **Base RTT: 4 / 8 / 12 us** (0.1 / 0.25 / 0.37% at 400G). This is the only
  part that depends on the tier. Each extra tier pair adds 4 us: two links out
  and two back.
- **Ramp: zero.**

DCQCN starts at `RC = RT = line rate`. A lone flow never queues more than a
packet behind itself, which is below the RED Kmin (12 KB at 100 G, 50 KB at
400 G; the RED rule is `queue_lossless_output.cpp:44-60`, applied on dequeue at
`:174`). No packet is marked, no CNP is generated, and DCQCN is identical to
RoCE in every single-flow cell, to the 0.1 us.

The parse.py floor counts payload bytes only. A loss-free RoCE transfer can
therefore never score below 1.016.

### 3.2 EQDS: headers plus a credit/packet mismatch, 1.031-1.037

EQDS sends 4,150 B packets, 64 B of header and 4,086 B of payload
(`eqds.cpp:392` sets the instance `_mtu` to `Packet::data_packet_size()`).
Headers alone cost 4,150 / 4,086 = **1.01566**.

The receiver's pull pacer grants 8 × 512 = 4,096 B of credit per pull
(`_credit_per_pull = 8`, `eqds.cpp:23`) and issues one pull every
`0.99 × 8 × pktByteTimes(mtu)`. The 0.99 is passed at
`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:550`, and `pktByteTimes` adds 38 B of Ethernet
framing. The pacer is constructed with the **static** `EqdsSrc::_mtu`, which
is still `_mss + _hdr_size` = 4,096 + 64 = 4,160 (`eqds.cpp:28`) at that point:
the pacers are built at `main_eqds.cpp:550`, before the first `EqdsSrc`
constructor runs at `:572`. The source spends 4,150 B of credit per packet.
Steady credit therefore covers 4,096 / (0.99 × 4,198) of the line, a cost of
**1.014653**.

Model: 1.01566 × 1.014653 = **1.030535** of the payload floor, plus the
base RTT. Against the measurements:

| | model (payload) | same ToR | same pod | cross pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 G | 13,831.6 us | +4.4 us | +8.4 us | +13.1 us |
| 400 G | 3,457.8 us | +4 us | +11 us | +21 us |

At 100 G the residual is exactly the base RTT (4 / 8 / 12 us). At 400 G it
is the RTT plus a small idle gap at startup: the first window lasts only
5.3 us at 400 G, so the source briefly waits for the first pulls.

**The first window is 64 packets, not 50 MTU.** The constructor sets
`_maxwnd = 50 * _mtu` (`eqds.cpp:351`) and `_credit_spec = _maxwnd`
(`:362`), but `main` then overwrites both: `setCwnd` and `setMaxWnd` are
called with `cwnd × data_packet_size()` (`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:573-574`),
with `cwnd = DEFAULT_CWND = 64` (`:33`, `:48`). `startFlow` resets
`_credit_spec = _maxwnd` (`eqds.cpp:969`). The speculative first window is
therefore 64 × 4,150 = 265,600 B. Checkpoint 1's "50 MTU, `eqds.cpp:351`"
was wrong. At 100 G that is 21 us of line-rate data; at 400 G, 5.3 us.

### 3.3 HPCC single flow: 1.049-1.059, η plus W_AI plus headers

HPCC now runs the paper's Algorithm 1 (`hpcc.cpp:585-586`:
`W = Wc / (U/η) + W_AI`), with η = 0.95 (`hpcc.cpp:38`),
W_AI = W_init (1 - η) / N (`:116`), W_init = B·T and N = 10 (`:40`).
Packets are 9,000 B of payload plus 64 B of header
(`datacenter/main_hpcc.cpp:54`), a 1.00711 header cost.

A lone flow's steady state follows from Algorithm 1. With Wc ≈ W, the fixed
point of `W = W·η/U + W_AI` is `U = η / (1 - W_AI/W)`. W itself sits near
η·B·T, so W_AI/W = (1 - η)/(N·η) = 0.526 % at **any** link speed and T. The
flow therefore runs at U = 0.95 / 0.99474 = 0.9550 of the line, and

    x = 1.00711 / 0.9550 = 1.0545 of the payload floor, plus the RTT.

| | model + RTT | same ToR | same pod | cross pod |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 G, T = 16.4 us | 14,157 / 14,161 / 14,165 us | 14,159 | 14,164 | **14,217** |
| 400 G, T = 13.1 us | 3,542 / 3,546 / 3,550 us | 3,543 | 3,547 | **3,519** |

Same ToR and same pod match the model to 1-3 us. Cross pod deviates in
opposite directions at the two speeds (+52 us at 100 G, -31 us at 400 G).
**Not established:** why. The cross-pod path is the only one with five INT
hops and the only one whose RTT is close to T, so the max-U hop and the
window-versus-RTT relation both differ from the shorter paths; neither was
traced (it needs `-hpcc_trace`).

So HPCC as specified costs about 5.5 % on an idle network: 5 % is the η
headroom the paper chooses on purpose, 0.7 % is headers, and the additive
step W_AI pushes U 0.5 % above η. RoCE and DCQCN pay 1.6 %, EQDS 3.1 %.

## 4. TP=8: the result is set by ECMP collisions, not by congestion control

### 4.1 The collisions

§4.1 and §4.2 use the seed-13 traces made for checkpoint 1
(`results/e1/paths.txt`, `results/e1/sinks/`, `results/e1/queues/`, legacy
`build/` binary, line numbers at `cab8d0c`). RoCE on the final `build_start`
binary is bit-identical to it at seed 13 in every cell (§1), so the traces
still describe the RoCE column. Seed 13 is not in the 1-5 table; §4.3 has the
per-seed classes.

Under `-strat ecmp_host`, every RoCE and DCQCN flow picks one fixed path
entropy, `_pathid = random() % 256` (`roce.cpp:66`). Every switch then picks
its uplink with `freeBSDHash(flow_id, pathid, _hash_salt) % n`. The hash is at
`datacenter/fat_tree_switch.cpp:339`, the per-switch salt is drawn at `:18`,
and uplinks are shuffled by `permute_paths` (`:308`, used at `:460`). All of
these draws come from one global mt19937 seeded by `-seed`, default 13
(`rng.cpp:7-24`, `datacenter/main_roce.cpp:317`). `-paths` is only syntax-checked
(`datacenter/main_roce.cpp:345`) and has no effect.

To find each flow's real path, `e1.sh trace` reran the matrix with one elephant
kept and the other seven shrunk to 4 KB. It then read off which queues are busy
for most of the run using `-log queue_usage`, the only text-mode logger
(`loggers.cpp:143-155`). Every isolated elephant ran at 1.017-1.02x, so no
single path is slow.

Results are in `results/e1/paths.txt`. For the 16-node default at seed 13:

    f1, f3  share US0->CS0 and CS0->US4                       (x2)
    f2, f4  share US1->CS1                                     (x2)
    f5, f7, f8 share US2->CS2 and CS2->US6; f7+f8 also enter
            US2 through the same ingress LS3->US2             (x3)
    f6      alone                                              (x1)
    L_max = 3

|---|---|---|
| ft128_1os | 3 | 3 |

### 4.2 Why RoCE equals exactly L_max × T

PFC plus FIFO is **work-conserving but port-fair, not flow-fair**. The
per-flow goodput time series (`e1.sh sinks`, `-log sink` at 10 us,
`results/e1/sinks/roce_default_tp8.csv`) on the 3-way bottleneck US2->CS2 shows:

- f5 arrives on its own ingress port (LS2->US2) and gets **195.6 Gb/s**, half
  the link.
- f7 and f8 arrive together on LS3->US2 and get **97.8 Gb/s** each, a quarter
  each.
- The bottleneck aggregate is 393.3-393.5 Gb/s, which is the full payload line
  rate (400 × 4000/4064 = 393.7). It drops below 95% of line rate in 1 of
  ~1,000 bins.

The mechanism is that each ingress `LosslessInputQueue` pauses its upstream
above 150 packets and resumes below 80 (`queue_lossless_input.cpp:70-72,
100-103`). The output queue is therefore fed in proportion to ingress ports,
not flows. f5 finishes at 2T. Then f7 and f8 finish their remaining halves at
full rate, at 3T = 10,227.6 + 12 = **10,240.0 us**. The 2-way pairs finish at
2T = 6,830 us. This gives the per-flow pattern
`1.020, 2.036 ×5, 3.052 ×2` (`results/e1/legacy/summary.csv`).

Because the bottleneck never idles, the slowest shard is
`L_max × T_single`, whatever the sharing rule:

| configuration | L_max | L_max × single | observed |
|---|---|---|---|
| default | 3 | 3 × 1.017 = 3.05 | 3.05 |
| ft128_1os | 3 | | 3.05 |
| 4os same pod | 2 | | 2.03 |
| 4os cross pod | 3 | | 3.05 |
| 8os same pod | 4 | | 4.065 |
| 8os cross pod | 5 | | 5.09 |

RoCE's seed-13 3.05x on the 16-node tree (checkpoint 1's headline) is therefore entirely an ECMP collision
result. There is no congestion inefficiency in it at all.

There are also no PFC victims. f6 shares no link with anyone, runs at 1.020x
under RoCE, and is never slowed by pause spreading. Whatever congestion control
might buy in other settings, in E1 there is nothing for it to rescue.


### 4.3 The placement is a lottery: classes per seed

RoCE's TP=8 result is `L_max × 1.017` on every seed and cell (per-seed table
in §2). Over seeds 1-5:

| cell | L_max, seeds 1-5 | forced by the topology |
|---|---|---|
| default, cross pod | 2 3 3 2 2 | no |
| ft128_1os, same pod and cross pod | 2 2 3 2 3 | no |
| ft128_4os, same pod | 2 2 3 3 2 | no (same pod never uses the 4:1 agg uplinks) |
| ft128_4os, cross pod | 3 3 3 5 4 | ≥ 2: 8 flows on the pod's 4 agg uplinks |
| ft128_8os, same pod | 4 4 4 4 4 | = 4: one ToR uplink carries 4 hosts |
| ft128_8os, cross pod | 4 6 5 6 4 | ≥ 4 |

On the 16-node tree the 3:2 split of 2x and 3x draws matches the
collision-class probabilities in `HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md` (2x 0.63, 3x 0.33);
the 10-seed reference sweep in CLAUDE.md gives six 2x and four 3x. Seed 13,
checkpoint 1's only seed, is a 3x draw.

The older 10-seed study (`results/e1/seeds_summary.csv`, legacy build, `-q 500`
on ft128) agrees for RoCE and EQDS. Its DCQCN row is the legacy transport and
is superseded by §5.

**Consequence for every later experiment.** The ECMP class moves the
slowest shard by 1.0x of floor or more, which is larger than any
congestion-control effect in this table except EQDS's spraying. Compare at
matched seeds. A difference between two topologies at one seed can be a
different hash draw: checkpoint 1's "4os same pod 2.03x against 1os 3.05x" was
exactly that, and over seeds 1-5 the two cells have identical medians (2.033).

## 5. DCQCN (paper RP + RED): slower than RoCE on every seed, and why

### 5.1 What the numbers say

- **Singles: identical to RoCE.** No queue, no mark (§3.1).
- **TP=8: 1.09-1.69x RoCE on every seed of every cell** (DCQCN / RoCE in the
  per-seed table, §2). The ratio is 1.42-1.69 on the 400 G tree, 1.09-1.40 on
  the 100 G ft128 trees.
- Only flows that share a link are hurt. In the traced default seed-1 run,
  the five uncollided flows finish at 3,421.6 us, exactly the RoCE single-flow
  time; the colliding ones at 7,620, 8,689 and 11,557 us. Against RoCE's
  port-fair FIFO, which keeps every shared link 100 % busy (§4.2), any
  idle time DCQCN introduces is pure loss.

### 5.2 Why: four mechanisms, measured

Traces: `DCQCN_TRACE=1` (one line per CNP and rate increase) on the default
and ft128_1os TP=8 cross-pod cells at seed 1, in
`results/e1/diag_final/dcqcn_trace/`. The mechanisms are the ones documented in
`HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md`, sections "DCQCN made faithful to the paper"
(Validation (b), the smoke workload) and "DCQCN switch marking:
probabilistic (RED) ECN" (Results (b)). The trace reproduces them on the E1
cells.

1. **Startup collapse: every colliding flow gets a CNP in the same 50 us
   window, repeatedly.** All 8 flows start at line rate (`RC = RT = rate`).
   Two line-rate flows on one link grow its queue at the full link rate, so
   it passes Kmax = 400 KB in 8 us at 400 G and 32 us at 100 G. Above Kmax
   every packet is marked (forced). The receiver sends one CNP per 50 us
   window per flow (`DCQCNSink::_cnp_interval`, `dcqcn.cpp:20`), so all
   colliding flows are cut together in the same window, and again in the
   next. With α = 1 (initial value, `dcqcn.cpp:55`), each CNP sets `RT = RC`
   and halves RC. Traced:
   - default, seed 1: CNPs at 20, 70, 120, 170 us (flow 1→9) and 25, 75,
     125 us (flow 0→8); they fall from 400 to **25 and 50 Gbps**
     (6-12 % of line) by 170 us.
   - ft128_1os, seed 1: CNPs at 34/84/134 and 46/96/146 us; both flows of the
     colliding pair fall from 100 to **12.5 Gbps** (12.5 %).
   Because `RT = RC` at each cut, the target rate the flow recovers *to* is
   also collapsed: fast recovery (`RC = (RT + RC)/2`, 5 periods) only climbs
   back to the last, already-halved rate.
2. **Slow additive recovery: R_AI = 0.1 % of line rate per 55 us.** This is
   the paper's value (OUR_CHANGES.md, "What was wrong", item 3;
   `dcqcn.cpp:61`). It is 400 Mbps per 55 us at 400 G, i.e. 7.3 Gbps per ms:
   going from 50 back to 200 Gbps (a fair half) takes about 20 ms, six times
   longer than an uncontended shard. Hyper increase needs both the 55 us timer
   and the byte counter (B = 10 MB, `dcqcn.cpp:27`) to have fired more than
   F = 5 times since the last CNP; at 25-50 Gbps a 10 MB byte-counter tick
   takes 1.6-3.2 ms, so hyper increase is rare (OUR_CHANGES.md: it needs
   about 60 MB sent since the last CNP, a third of a shard).
3. **Per-flow α stays near 1, so every later CNP is a deep cut.** α decays by
   (1 - 1/256) per 55 us without a CNP (`dcqcn.cpp:160`), i.e. it halves only
   every ~9.8 ms. Traced α at the end of the transfer: 0.45-0.60 on the 400 G
   tree, so the 5-8 CNPs per colliding flow each remove 22-30 % of the rate.
   Legacy DCQCN shared one static α that **every** flow's timer decayed
   (`dcqcn.cpp:228`, legacy branch), so with 8 flows it fell 8x as fast and
   later cuts were gentler (OUR_CHANGES.md: shared α 0.53 at 1.4 ms against
   0.92 per flow).
4. **RED marking is rate-proportional, but at these speeds it rarely gets the
   chance.** Under RED a flow's marks per CNP window grow with its packet
   count, so faster flows get more CNPs. That is the paper's convergence
   mechanism, and in quasi-steady state it works (OUR_CHANGES.md: rate/CNP
   correlation 0.36 under RED, 0.00 under step). But in E1 the marks are
   dominated by startup: the default seed-1 run made **1,235 forced marks
   (q > Kmax) and 19 probabilistic** ones out of 7,900 RED draws; ft128_1os
   seed 1 made 229 forced and 39 probabilistic out of 25,708 draws. A
   line-rate flow crosses the 350 KB RED band in about 10 us at 400 G, faster
   than one 50 us CNP window. After startup, RED's occasional
   desynchronised cuts turn one common finish time of the colliding flows
   into a spread (default seed 1: 7,620, 8,689, 11,557 us), and the TP metric
   reports the worst one.

The net effect: a colliding flow spends most of its transfer well below its
fair share, with no other flow able to use the gap, because the other flows
on that link were cut in the same windows. RoCE leaves the link full.

### 5.3 Why ft128 suffers less than the 16-node tree (1.09-1.40 against 1.42-1.69)

All three DCQCN step sizes scale with the line rate (R_AI, R_HAI are
fractions of it), so the recovery slope in *fraction of line per ms* is the
same at 100 G and 400 G: 0.1 % per 55 us = 1.8 % of line per ms. The collapse
depth is also similar (12.5 % of line at 100 G, 6-25 % at 400 G). But a
2x-class shard lasts 27 ms at 100 G and 6.8 ms at 400 G. The same collapse
and the same climb in milliseconds are therefore a 4x smaller share of the
transfer. The longer transfer also lets α decay: traced α at the end is
**0.15** on ft128_1os against 0.45-0.60 on the 16-node tree, so the 18-25
later CNPs on ft128 each remove only ~7 %. B = 10 MB is in bytes, not a
fraction of line rate, so hyper increase is no more frequent per unit of
transfer at 100 G.

The RED thresholds on 100 G trees (Kmin 12 KB, Kmax 400 KB) are untested
for fairness and queue behaviour (CLAUDE.md open item 6); these E1 numbers
are the first 100 G DCQCN-with-RED data.

### 5.6 What changed from checkpoint 1's DCQCN analysis

- The "rate sawtooth" of Appendix A.2 (cut, fast recovery, 20 Gbps

- The units bug that disabled hyper increase, the static α and the 5 % R_AI

  slower, not faster, on this workload.
- The `-linkspeed` inflation on ft128 (Appendix A.2) is gone: run.sh passes
  the topology's speed.

## 6. EQDS: the lead is packet spraying, the residual is hash imbalance

### 6.1 Spraying removes the ECMP collision

EQDS sprays every packet over `_no_of_paths` entropies, cycling an
XOR-permuted index (`EqdsSrc::nextEntropy`, `eqds.cpp:1238`; line numbers in
this subsection are HEAD, the grid below is legacy-build data). Its default is 64 in `main`
(`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:47`), and `-paths` sets it (`:270-273`, `:469`).
Every packet re-hashes at every switch. So no flow is pinned to a colliding
uplink, and the TP=8 transfer uses all 4 (or 16) core paths.

This is the main reason EQDS reaches 1.32x (default tree) and 1.17x (ft128)
where RoCE gets 2.04-3.05x. It is a load-balancing difference, not a
congestion-control one.

`e1.sh eqdsx` varies only the spray width. This grid was run for checkpoint 1
on the legacy `build/` binary at seed 13 (`results/e1/eqdsx_summary.csv`, one
deterministic run each); it was not rerun, but the final build reproduces the
default-tree EQDS seed study exactly (§6.2) and §6.3 repeats the `-paths 256`
point on ft128 with the final build:

| -paths | 1 | 2 | 4 | 16 | 64 | 256 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default, 400G | 2.21 (rtx 194k) | 2.22 (169k) | 1.48 (17k) | 1.24 (4.1k) | 1.22 (1.1k) | 1.15 (0) |
| ft128_1os, -linkspeed 100000 | 3.14 (451k) | 2.06 (211k) | 1.40 (59k) | 1.08 (8.4k) | 1.07 (7.5k) | 1.06 (3.9k) |

The rtx figures are retransmitted packets out of 328,488.

With one entropy, EQDS is an ECMP transport. It lands at 2.2-3.1x, with
hundreds of thousands of trimmed-and-retransmitted packets
(`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:57`: COMPOSITE queues trim instead of pausing).
That is no better than RoCE's collision result. **EQDS's advantage in this
table is per-packet spraying.** A fair "CC only" comparison would need RoCE
with spraying as well (`-strat ecmp_rr`), which E1 did not run.

Changing the buffer from `-q 500` to `-q 2000` changes little: 1.24 becomes
1.25 on the default tree, and 1.08 becomes 1.11 on ft128 (seed 13; §6.3 has
the final-build numbers).


### 6.2 Default tree, 1.24-1.34x: hash imbalance of a finite entropy set

Seeds 1-5 give 1.32, 1.32, 1.28, 1.24, 1.34, identical to the legacy seed
study, so the default-tree EQDS numbers did not move with the fixes. The
mechanism measured at seed 13 for checkpoint 1 still applies:

Each flow's 16 entropies map onto the 2 ToR uplinks and the 2 agg uplinks
through the same `freeBSDHash(flow_id, ev, salt)`. Sixteen draws over 2 choices
are rarely an even 8/8 split; 10/6 gives one uplink 62.5% of the flow.
`e1.sh queues` traced per-link busy fraction for the full matrix
(`results/e1/queues/eqds_default_tp8_p{16,256}.links`, legacy build, seed 13,
window 4,300 us):

- **16 entropies:** the 8 core-to-agg links are busy between **0.60 and 0.945**
  of the time. The slowest flow is limited by the link at 0.945.
- **256 entropies:** the spread narrows to **0.73-0.85**, and everything
  finishes by 3,854 us (1.15x).

Roughly 1.5% of the slowdown is the retransmission of trimmed packets
(4,236-17,516 of 328,488 packets over seeds 1-5). Another 3% is the per-flow
credit and header cost from §3.2. The rest is the max/mean load imbalance
across the sprayed links.

### 6.3 ft128 TP=8, 1.15-1.17x: the same imbalance, at the source ToR

On ft128_1os both TP=8 cells give 1.147-1.172 on every seed. Same pod and
cross pod are the same to 0.01, so the core tier plays no part. The
bottleneck is the source ToR's 4 uplinks. Seed 1, cross pod, busy fraction
over the transfer (`results/e1/diag_final/eqds_1os_tp8x_s1.links`, from
`-log queue_usage`):

| source ToR | to agg 0 | agg 1 | agg 2 | agg 3 | mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LS0 (flows 0-3) | 0.963 | 0.957 | 0.748 | 0.809 | 0.87 |
| LS1 (flows 4-7) | **0.498** | 0.992 | **0.996** | 0.993 | 0.87 |

The four shards from LS1 hash only about half their fair share of
entropies onto LS1->US0, so the other three uplinks run at 99-100 % and set
the finish time (flows 4-7 finish at 15,618-15,653 us, 1.166). Max/mean on
LS1's uplinks is 0.996 / 0.87 = 1.145, which with §3.2's 1.031 gives 1.18,
close to the measured 1.166. The destination downlinks sit at 0.87: the
receivers are not the bottleneck. EQDS has no mechanism that moves load
off a hot uplink, except that a retransmission picks the next entropy
(`sendRtxPacket`, `eqds.cpp:1342`, entropy at `:1364`).

Controls (`results/e1/diag_final/eqds_1os_tp8x_*`):

- **Wider spray fixes it.** `-paths 256` at `-q 2000`: seed 1 gives 1.06 with
  0 retransmissions, seed 3 gives 1.09.
- **Checkpoint 1's 1.075/1.084 was a lucky seed.** At seed 13 the matched-
  linkspeed run gives 1.084 at `-q 500` (8,351 retransmissions) and 1.107
  at `-q 2000` (5,241). Seeds 1-5 at `-q 500` give 1.16, 1.12, 1.17, 1.15,
  1.13, the same range as at `-q 2000`.
- **`-q 2000` against `-q 500`.** At seed 13 the deeper buffer is slightly
  slower (1.107 against 1.084) with fewer retransmissions. A plausible
  reading is that trimming at a shallow buffer re-sprays load off hot links
  (each retransmission takes the next entropy), so the deeper buffer
  balances less. Not traced.

### 6.4 The checkpoint 1 open question is moot

Checkpoint 1 asked why TP=8 EQDS on ft128 suffered less from the
`-linkspeed 400000` mismatch than single flows (Appendix A.3). With
`-linkspeed` read from the topology there is no mismatch. The ft128 singles
are 1.031-1.032 with 0 retransmissions, matching the §3.2 model to the RTT.
The minimum RTO at `-q 2000` and 100 G is
`150 + 2000 × 4,150 × 48 / 100e9` = 4,134 us (`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:411`),
far above any queueing delay here, and no run sent an RTS. Nothing is left
to explain.

### 6.5 Oversubscribed trees: EQDS completes and sits near the bound

With the stale-NACK abort removed, EQDS now finishes on 4os and 8os. The
lower bound for 8 shards through a cut is `(shards per bottleneck link) ×
1.0157`, since only header overhead applies on a fully busy link (the credit
pacer limits each receiver, not a shared upstream link).

| cell | bottleneck | bound | measured, median [range] | above bound | retransmissions per seed |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4os cross pod | 8 flows on the pod's 4 agg->core uplinks | 2 × 1.0157 = 2.031 | 2.089 [2.07-2.19] | +2.9 % | 154-193 k (0.5 per packet) |
| 8os same pod | 4 flows on each ToR's single uplink | 4 × 1.0157 = 4.063 | 4.210 [4.209-4.210] | +3.6 % | 763-768 k (2.3 per packet) |
| 8os cross pod | 4 per ToR uplink (forced), then the 2-per-agg uplinks | 4.063 | 4.364 [4.12-4.65] | +1.4 to +14 % | 0.5 k-777 k |
| 4os same pod | none (same pod never uses agg uplinks) | 1.0315 | 1.149 [1.09-1.21] | | 3-25 k |

- **4os cross pod.** The core uplinks are 97-99 % busy at seed 1
  (`eqds_4os_tp8x_s1.links`). Spraying cannot beat the 4:1 cut, but it does
  remove RoCE's extra collisions (RoCE 3.05-5.08).
- **8os same pod: the excess is exactly trimmed headers.** The 4 ToR uplinks
  are 100 % busy (`eqds_8os_tp8s_s1.links`). Each trimmed packet still
  sends its 64 B header across the bottleneck. 763,863 trims × 64 B on top of
  1,363.3 MB of wire bytes gives 4.063 × (1 + 763,863 × 64 / 1,363.3 MB)
  = 4.208, against 4.210 measured. Here EQDS loses to RoCE (4.065), which
  pauses instead of trimming and has a smaller header.
- **8os cross pod: imbalance plus wasted upstream capacity.** The two agg
  uplinks are unevenly loaded (1.000 against 0.918 at seed 1,
  `eqds_8os_tp8x_s1.links`). Packets trimmed at a later tier have already
  used capacity at the ToR uplink. The seed with 500 retransmissions is the
  best (4.12) and the one with 777 k the worst (4.65). EQDS still beats
  RoCE here (4.36 against 5.18 median) because RoCE adds hash collisions on
  top of the forced 4.
- **4os same pod** behaves like 1os (§6.3): imbalance at the source ToR.

## 7. HPCC (Algorithm 1): L_max × 1.06-1.10, with a multi-bottleneck penalty

HPCC is now deterministic per seed and shares RoCE's placements (same
`random()` draws), so its class is RoCE's class at the same seed. x per class
(HPCC x floor / RoCE L_max), per seed:

| cell | x / class, seeds 1-5 |
|---|---|
| default, cross pod | 1.10 1.06 1.06 1.07 1.10 |
| ft128_1os same pod | 1.07 1.07 1.08 1.07 1.08 |
| ft128_1os cross pod | 1.10 **1.35** 1.12 **1.35** 1.10 |
| ft128_4os same pod | 1.07 1.07 1.08 1.08 1.07 |
| ft128_4os cross pod | 1.09 1.10 1.10 1.09 1.10 |
| ft128_8os same pod | 1.08 (all seeds) |
| ft128_8os cross pod | **1.17** 1.09 1.12 1.10 **1.17** |

**The normal case: class × (η plus a little).** A link shared by k flows
with HPCC holding U near η finishes in about `k × 1.00711 / 0.95` = k × 1.060
of floor. The measured 1.06-1.10 per class is that plus 0-4 %, which was not
decomposed (one candidate is the U bias between flows on a shared link that
`HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md` measures, "What still differs from an ideal fluid
HPCC"). RoCE pays k × 1.017. So on an ECMP-pinned transfer HPCC is 5-8 %
slower than RoCE at the same seed outside the chain cases below, and never
faster: the η headroom has nothing to protect when there are no other
flows.

**Multi-bottleneck chains: 1.35.** ft128_1os cross pod, seeds 2 and 4. At
seed 2 the paths (`results/e1/diag_final/paths.txt`, one-elephant traces in
`diag_final/trace/`) are:

- f1 and f3 share LS0->US3 and US19->LS_16: a plain pair. They finish at
  2.06 and 2.19.
- f2, f4, f8 and f6 form a chain: f2-f4 share LS0->US1 and US17->LS_16,
  f4-f8 share US1->CS9 and CS9->US17, f8-f6 share LS1->US1 and US17->LS_17.

Max-min would give every chain flow half a link (RoCE: 2.03 for all). HPCC
reacts to the most-utilised hop on each flow's path, and a flow that crosses
two shared links sees the max of two U measurements. The middle flows f4 and
f8 therefore get less than half on their edge links, the end flows f2 and f6
more. The ends finish early (1.74 and 1.79), but while they run the middle
flows cannot fill the US1->CS9 link they share with each other, and they
finish at 2.50 and **2.71**. This is the paper's own statement that with
multiple bottlenecks HPCC converges to a Pareto-optimal allocation rather than
to max-min (HPCC paper Sec. 3.1; `HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md`, "What still differs
from an ideal fluid HPCC"). At a 1 KB MTU the same seed gives 2.50 instead
of 2.71 (`diag_final/hpcc_1os_tp8x_s2_mtu1000.*`): finer packets reduce the
measurement noise but do not remove the effect. The 8os cross-pod 1.17
seeds are the same kind of chain, through the 2:1 agg uplinks.

**Packet size is not the explanation for the per-class excess.** At a 1 KB
MTU (T = 12.5 us, `diag_final/hpcc_8os_*_mtu1000.*`) 8os same pod gets worse
(4.42 against 4.31 at 9 KB) and cross pod better (4.46 against 4.69, the
chain effect shrinking). In the rail incast (`docs/TOPOLOGY_RAIL.md`) the
shortfall below η grows as W_AI shrinks, also independent of packet size;
the exact mechanism was not traced.

**Fidelity notes.** Checkpoint 1's HPCC column (Appendix A.4) measured a
diverging window and wall-clock-random placements; neither applies now.
HPCC's PFC thresholds are still the binary default (15/12 packets of 9 KB),
not matched to RoCE's 80/150 of 4 KB. In steady state Algorithm 1 keeps
queues far below either (validation: 18-54 KB); whether PFC fires during
the line-rate start was not checked.

## 9. What the logs can and cannot tell you, and which `-log` to use

**Free text on stdout:**

- RoCE/DCQCN print only `Flow ... finished at` and a final `New: ... Rtx: ...`.
  Rtx is always 0 under PFC. DCQCN with RED also prints one `ECN_RED stats:`
  line (packets drawn, probabilistic and forced marks). For CNPs and rates,
  set `DCQCN_TRACE=1` (one line per CNP and per rate increase, used in §5.2);
  `ECN_TRACE=1` prints every mark.
- EQDS prints per-flow finish lines, plus `New/Rtx/RTS/Bounced` totals
  (`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:683`). This is the only built-in loss signal, and
  it is what diagnosed the checkpoint 1 `-linkspeed` problem (Appendix A.3)
  and the trimming arithmetic in §6.5.
- HPCC prints the window per ACK only with `-hpcc_trace` (Algorithm 1
  build); the legacy binary printed `CWND1/CWND2` on every ACK
  (Appendix A.4), which is why old HPCC logs are large.

**The `-log` options** (`datacenter/main_roce.cpp:155-179`,
`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:197-224`):

| option | output | sampling | use |
|---|---|---|---|
| `queue_usage` | text to stdout (`loggers.cpp:143-155`) | per-queue busy time, 10 us | **The cheapest way to find which links a flow uses and which ones saturate.** Used for `paths.txt`, §6.2, §6.3 and §6.5. |
| `sink` (+ `-logtime`) | binary to `-o` | per-flow cumulative ACK | **Per-flow goodput time series.** This is the only way to see the port-fair split (§4.2) and legacy DCQCN's sawtooth (Appendix A.2); for DCQCN rates `DCQCN_TRACE=1` is cheaper. Recommended `-logtime 0.01` (10 us). |
| `tor_upqueue` / `tor_downqueue` | binary | queue length every 10 us | Despite the names, these attach to all queues (`datacenter/main_roce.cpp:386-389`). Use them for queue occupancy (Appendix A.2.1). |
| `switch`, `traffic` | binary | per packet | Very large, and not needed for E1. |

The binary loggers need `parse_output <file> -ascii`, plus `idmap.txt`
(written to the cwd) to turn numeric IDs into queue and flow names. Run each
simulation in its own directory, as `run.sh` and `e1.sh` do, or concurrent runs
overwrite each other's `logout.dat` and `idmap.txt`.

**Recommendations for later experiments:**

1. Always add `-log sink -logtime 0.01` to any run whose result needs a
   mechanism.
2. Add `-log queue_usage` when load balancing is in question.
3. Record `-seed` and report results over at least 5 seeds, paired across
   policies. A single seed
   reports one ECMP placement, as §4.3 shows.

## 10. Summary of findings

All numbers: final transports, seeds 1-5, medians unless stated.

1. **Uncontended single flows cost 1.6-5.9 %, all of it explained.** RoCE and
   DCQCN pay headers (1.6 %) plus the base RTT; there is no ramp and no CNP.
   EQDS pays headers (1.57 %) plus a 1.47 % credit shortfall from the pacer's
   static 4,160 B MTU, 1.031 in total, plus the RTT. Its first window is 64
   packets (265.6 KB), not 50 MTU. HPCC pays η = 0.95 by design, plus 0.7 %
   headers, minus the 0.5 % W_AI pushes U above η: 1.055 on same-ToR and
   same-pod paths (cross pod differs by +52 / -31 us, not traced).
2. **TP=8 under per-flow ECMP is set by the hash.** RoCE is exactly
   L_max × 1.017 on every seed. L_max is 2 or 3 on 1:1 trees (3:2 over
   seeds 1-5), forced to ≥ 2 on 4os cross pod and to 4 on 8os. Checkpoint 1's
   3.05 was a seed-13 3-way draw; the median placement gives 2.03-2.04.
3. **DCQCN (paper RP + RED) is 1.09-1.69x RoCE on every seed of every TP=8
   cell, and identical on singles.** Colliding flows all get a CNP in the same
   50 us window at startup and are cut to 6-25 % of line within 170 us; the
   paper's R_AI (0.1 % per 55 us) needs about 20 ms to climb back; per-flow α
   stays near 1 so later CNPs cut deep; and at 98-99 % forced marks RED's
   rate-proportional marking barely acts. The penalty is smaller on 100 G
   trees (1.09-1.40) because the same collapse is a smaller share of a 4x
   longer transfer and α has time to decay. No K, step or RED, beats RoCE;
   step K = 400 never marks and equals it. The checkpoint 1 legacy DCQCN is
   faster than the faithful one (2.45 against 3.44 median) only because of
   its 50x R_AI and shared α.
4. **EQDS's lead is packet spraying.** 1.32 on the 16-node tree and 1.17 on
   ft128 against RoCE's 2.04 and 2.03. The residual over 1.031 is hash
   imbalance of 16 entropies at the source ToR's uplinks (one uplink at 0.50
   while three are at 0.99); `-paths 256` brings ft128 to 1.06-1.09. On
   oversubscribed trees it sits 3-7 % above the cut's bound because trimmed
   packets still spend header bytes on the bottleneck (8os same pod:
   4.063 × (1 + trims × 64 B / bytes) = 4.208, measured 4.210). It beats RoCE
   on 4os and 8os cross pod, and loses on 8os same pod (4.21 against 4.065).
5. **HPCC (Algorithm 1) is class × 1.06-1.10, 5-8 % behind RoCE at the same
   seed, never ahead.** Where flows form a chain of shared links (ft128_1os
   cross pod, seeds 2 and 4; 8os cross pod, seeds 1 and 5) it converges to a
   Pareto-optimal rather than max-min allocation, the middle flows lose, and
   the slowest shard rises to class × 1.17-1.35.
6. **Nothing here needs congestion control.** With no PFC victims and a
   work-conserving FIFO, every rate-based controller can only remove
   bandwidth from a TP=8 transfer. The only lever that wins in E1 is load
   balancing (EQDS's spraying). Later experiments that add competing traffic
   are where the controllers can earn their cost.

### A.2 Legacy DCQCN (pre-fidelity: shared α, packet byte counter, R_AI 5 %, step marking)

#### A.2.1 The loss is bottleneck utilisation, nothing else

The slowest shard is `L_max × S_wire / (u × C)`, where u is the time-averaged
utilisation of the bottleneck link. From the sink series
(`results/e1/sinks_summary.txt`, default tree, seed 13):

| | 3-way bottleneck aggregate | below 95% of line rate | slowest shard |
|---|---|---|---|
| RoCE | 393.5 Gb/s | 1/1,022 bins | 10,240 us |
| DCQCN K=100 | 333.2 Gb/s | 760/1,208 bins | 12,098 us |

The ratio 393.5 / 333.2 = 1.181 equals 12,098 / 10,240 = 1.181. All of
DCQCN's penalty is idle capacity on the bottleneck link. The 2-way pairs
average 334-339 Gb/s, which explains why they finish at 2.35-2.40x instead of
2.04x.

The queue samples confirm it (`e1.sh queues`, `-log tor_upqueue` at 10 us,
`results/e1/queues/*.txt`):

| queue | policy | mean occupancy | empty |
|---|---|---|---|
| US2->CS2 | RoCE | 168 packets | 0% of samples |
| US2->CS2 | DCQCN | 30.6 packets | 16% of samples |
| LS3->US2 | RoCE | 234 packets (PFC-bounded, max 346) | |
| US0->CS0, US1->CS1 | DCQCN | 15 packets | 45% of samples (about 12% while their flows were still active) |

An empty bottleneck queue means the link idles. RoCE never lets it empty.

#### A.2.2 Why DCQCN under-fills the link: the rate sawtooth

The marking threshold is `K = ecn_threshold × MSS`
(`datacenter/main_roce.cpp:335`), and packets are marked on dequeue when the
output queue exceeds K (`queue_lossless_output.cpp:121-122`).

The receiver sends at most one CNP per 50 us per flow (`dcqcn.cpp:19`,
`208-229`). Each CNP sets `RT = RC` and `RC *= (1 - α/2)` (`dcqcn.cpp:54-60`).
α starts at 1 (`dcqcn.cpp:22`) and moves by g = 1/256 per event (`:23`, `:57`,
`:130-134`). For the whole of these transfers α is therefore close to 1, so
**each CNP roughly halves the rate**.

The sink series shows this directly. At ~120 us both members of a colliding
pair go from ~198 to ~48 Gb/s, which is two CNPs in a row. Their aggregate,
~96 Gb/s, is a quarter of the link, and the queue drains within tens of us.

Recovery is timer-driven, every 55 us (`dcqcn.cpp:20`, `137-143`):

- **Fast recovery** for F = 5 periods (`dcqcn.cpp:26`, `84-87`), with
  `RC = (RT + RC)/2`.
- **Additive increase** after that, with `RT += R_AI`, where
  R_AI = rate/20 = 20 Gb/s at 400G (`dcqcn.cpp:42`, `93-97`).
- **No hyper-increase.** Hyper-increase needs both the timer count and the
  byte-counter count above F (`dcqcn.cpp:88`). But `_byte_counter`
  accumulates `_highest_sent`, which RoCE counts in **packets**, against
  `_B = 10,000,000`, which is meant as bytes (`dcqcn.cpp:24`, `120-123`). The
  byte counter therefore never fires (41,944 < 10^7), and `_BC` stays 0.

So climbing from 48 back to ~198 Gb/s takes ~620 us. The next cut comes at
~920 us and drops the pair to ~102 Gb/s. The link spends most of each cycle
below capacity: 760 of 1,208 bins under 95%.

On the 3-way bottleneck, DCQCN converges to flow fairness: f5, f7 and f8 each
get 83-89 Gb/s in the first ms, and all three finish at ~12.0-12.1 ms. That is
fairer than RoCE's 1/2-1/4-1/4 split. Fairness does not help the slowest shard,
though, because the slowest shard is bounded by total bytes divided by
utilisation.

#### A.2.3 The threshold sweep: DCQCN is never better than RoCE

16-node default, TP=8 cross-pod, `-dcqcn K` with K in packets, 3 reps each.
The runs are deterministic, so every spread is 0%. Data is in
`results/e1/sens_summary.csv`, logs in `results/e1/dcqcn_sens_k<K>_r<r>.log`,
parsed by parse.py with C = 400.

| K (packets) | 5 | 10 | 25 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 | 300 | 400 | RoCE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| slowest shard (us) | 12,268 | 12,221 | 11,882 | 12,135 | 12,098 | 12,343 | 11,452 | 10,499 | 10,240 | 10,240 |
| x floor | 3.66 | 3.64 | 3.54 | 3.62 | 3.61 | 3.68 | 3.41 | 3.13 | **3.05** | 3.05 |

The curve has three regions:

- **K = 5-150: insensitive (3.54-3.68).** The size of each cut is set by
  α ≈ 1, not by K. A lower K only makes the marks start a little earlier.
- **K = 200-300: approaching RoCE.** The PFC-bounded output queue (max 320-346
  packets under RoCE, see §5.1) only occasionally exceeds K.
- **K = 400: identical to RoCE, to the microsecond.** The queue can never reach
  400 packets, because PFC pauses the ingress at 150 per port. No packet is
  ever marked.

The best DCQCN setting is "never mark", which is RoCE. The paired seed study
(§4.3) shows the same thing for every one of 15 placements: DCQCN is slower by
1.17-1.31x. This is not a tuning problem. In a fabric with no PFC victims and a
work-conserving FIFO, rate-based congestion control can only remove
bandwidth.

#### A.2.4 Sharpening factors in this build

Three properties of the build make the penalty larger than a faithful DCQCN
would:

- The `_alpha` variable is a single **static** shared by all flows
  (`dcqcn.h:44`, `dcqcn.cpp:22`). A CNP to any flow raises everyone's α, and
  every flow's timer decays it.
- There is no hyper-increase (the units bug in §5.2).
- R_AI is 5% of line rate. The paper scales R_AI to 0.1%.

An uncommitted working-tree rewrite of `dcqcn.cpp` by another agent changes
all three. Its results will differ, and this section describes the `build/`
binary.

#### A.2.5 128-node DCQCN is worse still, because of `-linkspeed`

With `-linkspeed 400000` on 100G links, `_link`, `RC` and `RT` start at
400 Gb/s (`dcqcn.cpp:36-43`). The source sends into a 100G host queue. That
queue is a FairPriorityQueue at the tier-0 speed
(`datacenter/fat_tree_topology.cpp:1151-1162`). Its drop is disabled, and it
passes pauses back to the source (`queue.cpp:434-466`).

R_AI becomes 20 Gb/s, which is 20% of the real link per 55 us. The result is
a much harsher oscillation. At `-linkspeed 100000` (`ctl_summary.csv`) DCQCN
improves from 3.999 to 3.641 (cross pod) and from 3.915 to 3.578 (same pod).
It is still 1.17-1.19x RoCE. RoCE is unaffected by the flag: 3.049 either way,
because the host queue serialises at 100G regardless.

### A.3 Legacy EQDS on ft128: the `-linkspeed` misconfiguration and the abort

#### A.3.1 The ft128 EQDS singles (1.31-1.33x) are the `-linkspeed` misconfiguration

`run.sh` passes `-linkspeed 400000` on 100G topologies. Two things in EQDS are
derived from that flag:

- **The receiver's pull pacer** runs at it (`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:550`,
  `:585`). Each receiver therefore issues credit at about 4× what its 100G
  downlink can drain.
- **The minimum RTO** is
  `150 us + q × 6 × 8 / linkspeed` = 399 us (`datacenter/main_eqds.cpp:411`).
  That is too short for queues that actually drain at 100G.

The logs show the result. At `-linkspeed 400000` a single flow sends
**12,757 retransmissions** on top of 41,061 new packets, plus 114 RTS. RTS are
sent from the RTO path, `eqds.cpp:1510-1566`. At `-linkspeed 100000` the same
flow sends 0 retransmissions.

The completion time is exactly the retransmission inflation:
(41,061 + 12,757) / 41,061 × 13,632 us = 17,867 us, against 17,858 observed.
The cross-pod run gives 17,598 predicted against 17,605 observed. The
ft128 EQDS single-flow row measures this misconfiguration, not EQDS. The
matched-linkspeed value is **1.032x** (§3.2).

**Not established:** why the TP=8 ft128 runs suffer much less from the same
mismatch, 9,986 retransmissions (3%) and 1.075x. One guess is that the 8
senders interleave on shared links so each one's over-credit is diluted. That
was not tested.

#### A.3.2 EQDS aborts on the oversubscribed trees and on narrow sprays

With the `build/` binary, EQDS aborted (rc 134) in these runs:

- ft128_4os at ls400
- ft128_8os at both linkspeeds (only 1 of 8 flows finished at ls100)
- ft128_1os at ls400 with `-paths` 1, 2 and 4

ft128_1os at ls400 with `-q 2000` stalls with 4 of 8 flows finished.

The backtrace is `EqdsSrc::processNack` → `abort()` at `eqds.cpp:876`: a NACK
arrives for a sequence number that is no longer in `_tx_bitmap`. That happens
when the RTO path has already erased the send record (`eqds.cpp:1526`) and
re-queued it. In other words, the abort is downstream of the spurious RTOs
from §6.3, which is why at matched linkspeed 4os completes.

At ls100, ft128_4os completes at **2.08x** with 303,939 retransmissions. That is
close to the ideal of 2.0 for 8 flows through 4 agg uplinks at 4:1, so on 4os
EQDS also loses its spraying advantage and is held at the oversubscription
bound. Commit `b4832e8` replaced the abort with an ignore, but that commit is
not in `build/`.

### A.4 Legacy HPCC (compounding window, wall-clock reseed)

#### A.4.1 HPCC single flow: 1.017-1.061, an artefact

The HPCC numbers are an artefact of the controller code, not of HPCC.

`htsim_hpcc` uses 9,000 B packets (`datacenter/main_hpcc.cpp:54`), so headers
cost only 0.71%. Its 1x-wire time is 18,642 × 9,064 B × 8 / 400G = 3,379.4 us.

- **Same ToR (1.061x).** The CWND trace (the log prints `CWND1`/`CWND2` on every
  ACK, `hpcc.cpp:193-199`) converges to 570,077 B, which is 0.95 × the initial
  600 KB window. Pacing is `W/T` (`hpcc.cpp:202`, T = 12 us), so the flow is
  paced at exactly η = 0.95 of line rate (`hpcc.cpp:30`). Then
  3,379.4 / 0.95 + 4 = 3,561 us, which matches the measurement. This is HPCC
  working as designed: it leaves 5% headroom.
- **Same pod and cross pod (1.017-1.018).** These run at line rate because the
  controller diverges. In the cross-pod log, CWND grows by a factor of about
  1.12 per ACK from ~3 MB at 147 us to 4.3×10^10 B by 163 us, and exceeds 2^63
  in 15% of all ACKs. The W/T pacing then sits far above C, and the 400G host
  queue simply serialises at line rate. The window never binds.

Two code defects explain why HPCC as built can diverge. The paper and the
IETF draft quoted in the source comments compute
`W = Wc/(U/η) + W_AI` against a reference window `Wc` that is updated once per
RTT, and they refresh the per-link INT baseline on every ACK
("`R = W/T; L = ack.L`", quoted at `hpcc.cpp:190`). The code departs from this
in two ways:

1. `processAck` assigns the result to `_cwnd` on every ACK in both branches
   (`hpcc.cpp:193,198`). `computeWind` then divides that `_cwnd` again on the
   next ACK (`hpcc.cpp:401-402`), so the multiplicative step compounds per ACK
   instead of applying once per RTT. Once `_incStage >= 5` (`hpcc.cpp:401`),
   every ACK multiplies the window by η/U. With U below η that factor is
   greater than 1, and the window grows exponentially.
2. `_link_info` is written only when the hop count changes, which in practice
   means on the first ACK (`hpcc.cpp:376-379`). It is never refreshed
   afterwards. So `txRate` is the average since the first ACK, `tau` is pinned
   at T, and the queue term `min(qlen_now, qlen_first_ack)` is about 0
   (`hpcc.cpp:360-373`). HPCC as built cannot see queues at all.

With a single INT hop (same ToR), the measured utilisation equals the flow's
own pacing rate, and the compounding has a fixed point at η. With 3 or 5 hops
it does not converge. **Not established:** why U stays below η on the
multi-hop path. Settling that needs an instrumented build, which E1 was not
allowed to make.

#### A.4.2 HPCC TP=8: the 58% spread was the ECMP lottery

The HPCC binary seeds the global RNG from `time(NULL)` (`hpcc.cpp:65`) right
before drawing `_pathid` (`hpcc.cpp:66`), so every run gets a fresh ECMP
placement. Per-flow x floor by rep (`results/e1/hpcc_default_e1_tp8_crosspod_r*.csv`):

    r1: 2.02 2.03 2.02 2.03 1.37 2.13 1.02 1.26     -> 2-way collisions
    r2: 1.02 1.89 2.15 2.15 1.02 2.03 2.02 1.02     -> 2-way
    r3: 1.02 2.94 2.61 3.20 2.61 3.20 2.94 1.02     -> 3-way
    r4: 2.02 2.03 2.02 2.03 2.02 1.02 1.02 2.03     -> 2-way
    r5: 1.02 1.02 2.02 2.03 2.02 2.03 2.02 2.03     -> 2-way

Every HPCC result is `L_max × ~1.01`, just like RoCE:

- 2-way colliding pairs finish together at 2.02-2.03.
- The one 3-way draw (r3) gives 3.20, which is slightly worse than RoCE's 3.05
  for the same L_max.

The HPCC median of 2.13x versus RoCE's 3.05x reflects four lucky draws out of
five, not an HPCC advantage. When the placements match, HPCC is no better.
Given §3.3 (its window does not bind on multi-hop paths), HPCC as built
behaves essentially like PFC-paced RoCE with 9 KB packets.

HPCC's buffers are also not matched to the others. `run.sh` passes no
`-pfc_thresholds` for HPCC, so it keeps 15/12 packets of 9 KB
(`datacenter/main_hpcc.cpp:67`), about 135 KB, against 600 KB for RoCE. A
HEAD rebuild has `srand(time)` removed (`2521a60`). HPCC then becomes
deterministic per seed and should be compared on the same seeds as §4.3.

### A.6 Why legacy DCQCN was run directly with a short `-end`

The `roce.cpp:329` bug keeps every RoCE and DCQCN source's pacing chain alive
until `-end`. DCQCN additionally spawns an extra event chain per rate update
(`dcqcn.cpp:75,147`). At `-end 2000000`, the 128-node DCQCN runs took hours.

Shortening `-end` changes no result, because every flow finishes long before
the new end:

- K=400 at `-end 2000000` gives 10,240.0 us, the same as at 20,000.
- The old 2 s 128-node single-flow logs gave 13,650.5, 13,645.8 and 13,641.2 us,
  identical to the direct runs.
- The `-log sink` K=100 run at 20 ms reproduces the smoke-test per-flow times
  exactly.

`e1.sh` records the exact command in each `.out` file.
