# E3: congestion control vs offered load

**Question.** How far from the theoretical floor does each congestion-control
policy put a KV-cache transfer as offered load rises from 30% to 90%?

**Status.** RoCE (PFC only, no CC), HPCC, EQDS and DCQCN done on both
fabrics, 3 seeds, every run complete and with zero lossless violations.

Metrics, all from `scripts/exp_report.py`, per request (slowest shard, never
the mean):

- `x_floor` = request completion / floor.
- `ideal` = completion in a fluid, max-min fair fabric whose aggregate
  resources (host up/down, ToR up/down groups, pod up/down groups) are
  perfectly balanced. It is computed from the matrix and the topology alone,
  so it is the same for every policy. `x_ideal` = completion / ideal: 1.0
  means the policy wasted nothing that a perfect transport could have
  saved. The ideal is not 1.0 at high load because requests overlap.
- Deadline miss: completion > start + 2 x floor.
- Jain's index over completion / floor (prompts are all 4,096 tokens, so this
  is the same as normalising by prompt length).

## Why (RoCE)

### 1. RoCE has no congestion control, so the fabric is only as good as the ECMP hash

RoceSrc sends at line rate from the first packet to the last: `_pacing_rate`
is set once to the link rate (roce.cpp:78), `update_spacing` derives the
packet gap from it (roce.h:48), and the send loop (roce.cpp:310-344) has
neither a window nor a rate controller; it stops only when PFC pauses it
(roce.cpp:321). Each flow picks one path id for its lifetime
(`_pathid = random() % 256` once per source, roce.cpp:66, stamped on every
packet, roce.cpp:294) and the switch maps it with
`freeBSDHash(flow_id, pathid, _hash_salt)` (fat_tree_switch.cpp:339, salt
`random()` at line 18). If two flows hash onto the same 400 G (or 100 G)
link they each get half of it until one finishes, and nothing moves either
of them.

That is why x_floor comes in quantized steps. A TP = 8 request alone on ft16
(`kv_llama70b_4k_tp8.cm`, E6) gives shards at exactly 1.02 / 2.03 / 3.05 x
floor (1.016 = 64 B header per 4,000 B packet, roce.cpp:36 and
rocepacket.h:46, 68). On ft128 at load 30, seed 1, the per-flow
distribution is 14 flows at 1x, 33 at 2x, 23 at 3x and 2 at 4x, the 2x and 3x
classes being shards that shared a ToR uplink with one or two others. The
ideal at load 30 is 1.00 on both fabrics, so the whole p50 of 3.0-3.5 at
low load is ECMP collisions, not load. The ToR uplinks are the only class
that is paused much at 30% (0.17 on ft16, 0.25 on ft128). On ft16 the two
uplinks of one ToR are visibly unequal: `LS0->US0` utilization 0.43 vs
`LS0->US1` 0.75 at load 70, 0.54 vs 0.26 at load 30.

### 2. Deadline miss is 100% at every load: one collision is enough

The deadline is 2 x floor and a two-way collision costs 2.03 x floor, so
a request misses as soon as ANY of its 8 shards shares a link with one
other flow. On ft128 a TP = 8 instance occupies two ToRs, each putting 4
flows onto 4 uplinks: the chance that 4 independent hashes land on 4
different uplinks is 4! / 4^4 = 9.4% per ToR, under 1% for both, before the
agg uplinks and core downlinks get their own chance to collide. On ft16
each ToR puts 2 flows on 2 uplinks (50% chance of a collision per ToR, 4
source ToRs). So every request misses, at 30% load as at 90%, while the
ideal fabric misses none at 30% (and none at any load on ft128). On ft16 the
ideal also misses 60-89% from 50% load upward: all requests share the same
8 host pairs, so overlapping requests split the NICs even in a perfect
fabric. The deadline miss rate cannot separate RoCE's loads; x_floor and
x_ideal can.

### 3. Above 30%, requests overlap and the host NIC turns RoCE into processor sharing

When a second request arrives before the first finishes, both RoceSrc
instances on a host push packets into the same NIC queue. That queue is a
FairPriorityQueue whose per-priority queues are `FairPullQueue`s
(queue.h:178), i.e. per-flow round robin, and whose drop is commented out
(queue.cpp:480-484), so it never sheds load: at load 70 on ft16 host
queues reach about 220,000 packets (0.9 GB) while each source keeps sending
at line rate. Every concurrent request on a host therefore gets an equal
slice of the NIC for as long as they overlap. Under Poisson arrivals at 50%+
load the 8 NICs of ft16 are busy for long stretches, and every request
in a busy period is slowed by the number of requests sharing it: p50 rises
3.46 -> 6.87 -> 8.65 -> 14.41 x floor. The ideal fabric is also processor
sharing, which is why x_ideal grows much more slowly (3.46 -> 4.28 at the
median) than x_floor: most of the extra delay at high load would be there in
a perfect network too. What RoCE adds on top is the collision factor of
point 1, compounded: each overlap period runs at the speed of that request's
worst link.

ft128 behaves the same way but more gently: 8 prefill instances share the
load, so busy periods are shorter, the ideal stays at 1.0-1.07 x floor, and
RoCE goes 3.04 -> 6.17. All the ft128 excess is network waste
(x_ideal = x_floor up to load 70).

### 4. PFC pauses and head-of-line blocking

Pauses are where collisions turn into idle links. A LosslessInputQueue sends
PAUSE upstream when its accounted bytes pass 150 packets and RESUME below 80
(queue_lossless_input.cpp:70-72, 100-102; thresholds main_roce.cpp:345). The
accounting is released only when the packet leaves the switch's egress
(queue_lossless_output.cpp:152), so an ingress port full of packets for one
congested uplink also stops packets that wanted a free uplink. A paused
LosslessOutputQueue stops all its traffic; there is one traffic class
(queue_lossless_output.cpp:50-57, 170). That is head-of-line blocking at
the port level.

At the host it is worse. When the ToR pauses a host, the NIC's
FairPriorityQueue forwards the PAUSE to every RoceSrc on that host
(queue.cpp:461-466; sources are attached with `addHostSender`,
main_roce.cpp:573) and stops its own service (queue.cpp:516-519). One
request's shard waiting behind a collided uplink therefore stalls the
other requests' shards from the same host, even if their paths are idle.
The diagnostics show this as the dominant pause: host NICs are paused for
26-34% of the time they hold packets on ft16 and 47% on ft128 at load 70
(5% on ft128 at load 30, where requests rarely overlap on a host), ToR
uplinks 7-29%. Pauses barely propagate past the ToR on ft16 (agg and
core 0.00) because a K = 4 agg has only 2 ingress ports from ToRs. On ft128,
with 4 hosts and 4 aggs per ToR, they reach the agg uplinks (0.10) and core
downlinks (0.07) at load 70.

So RoCE's waste has two parts: links idle while a collided neighbour
drains (pause share times idle capacity), and requests coupled through
shared NIC pauses. At 90% on ft16 the ToR-uplink pause share drops to 0.07
because the fabric is busy enough that most links have something to send;
x_ideal at the median is still 4.3.

### 5. Jain's index stays high because RoCE is uniformly bad

Jain is 0.91-0.98 at every load on both fabrics. With fixed 4,096-token
prompts it only measures spread across requests, and processor sharing on
the NICs plus a near-certain collision per request make every request
similarly slow. The p99 / p50 ratio (1.2-1.7) says the same. RoCE is not
unfair to particular requests; it is slow for all of them. Jain should
separate policies that serve requests in different orders (EQDS, and the
scheduling axis later), not this one.

**Answer for RoCE:** 3.0-3.5 x floor already at 30% load, all of it ECMP
collisions (the ideal is 1.0). It rises to 6.2 (ft128) and 14.4 (ft16) at 90%
as requests overlap and the host NIC shares them out, with PFC pauses
coupling requests at the host. 100% deadline miss everywhere, Jain >= 0.91.

## Why (HPCC)

HPCC here is the paper-faithful sender of `build_hpccfix`
(HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md, "HPCC: from a compounding window to the paper's
Algorithm 1"): per ACK `W = Wc / (U / eta) + W_AI` with Wc updated once per
RTT and W clamped to [MSS, W_init] (`computeWindPaper`, hpcc.cpp:583-601),
pacing W / T (`setWindow`, hpcc.cpp:603-610), eta = 0.95 (hpcc.cpp:38),
W_init = B x T (hpcc.cpp:91, 107) and W_AI = W_init (1 - eta) / N with the
default N = 10 (hpcc.cpp:40, 116): 655,000 B / 3,275 B at 400 G and 13.1 us,
205,000 B / 1,024 B at 100 G and 16.4 us. U is computed from the INT every
switch egress stamps on data packets (queue length, timestamp, tx bytes,
link rate; queue_lossless_output.cpp:186-201). N = 2 is a sensitivity point
(one load level below), not the reported configuration.

### 1. The collision classes stay, each 5% slower than RoCE's

HPCC controls the rate on a path but does not choose the path. Each source
draws one path id for its lifetime (`_pathid = random() % 256`, hpcc.cpp:81),
stamps it on every packet (hpcc.cpp:390), and the switch hashes it
(fat_tree_switch.cpp:339), exactly as RoCE does. On ft128 at load 30 (ideal
1.00, almost no host overlap: 2 same-sender overlapping request pairs in 3
seeds), the per-flow distribution is RoCE's: rounding each flow's x_floor to
multiples of its single-flow value, seeds 1 / 2 / 3 give 22 / 24 / 27 flows
in the 1x class, 30 / 35 / 23 in 2x, 14 / 9 / 17 in 3x and 6 / 4 / 5 in 4x and
above (RoCE: 14 / 23 / 22, 33 / 33 / 30, 23 / 12 / 16, 2 / 4 / 4). The 1x
class sits at exactly 1.059 x floor (10 flows on seed 1), which is
1 / (eta x 9,000 / 9,064) = 1.060: HPCC deliberately keeps 5% of every
bottleneck free, where RoCE's lone flows run at 1.017. Per request, RoCE's
slowest shards sit on the class values (2.04, 3.05), HPCC's are spread
between 2.20 and 5.57 and none sits at 2.12: when a competitor finishes,
the survivor speeds up (HPCC's fast convergence), and a shard crossing two
shared links sees max(U_A, U_B) and is squeezed below its max-min share
(the parking-lot sub-class, HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md HPCC validation b). The
net is p50 3.18 vs RoCE 3.04 on ft128 (+5%, the eta factor) and 3.72 vs 3.46
on ft16 at load 30. Deadline miss is 1.00 at every load on both fabrics for
the same reason as RoCE's (point 2 above): a two-way collision costs
2 / (0.95 x 0.993) = 2.12 x floor > 2.

### 2. INT removes PFC from the picture

HPCC runs on the same lossless (PFC) queues as RoCE (`queue_type qt =
LOSSLESS_INPUT`, main_hpcc.cpp:62), with HPCC's default thresholds of
15 / 12 packets of 9 KB (main_hpcc.cpp:67, 135 KB per ingress port). Yet the
pause share is 0.000-0.005 on every queue class at load 70 on both fabrics
(table above), against RoCE's 0.34 / 0.12 (ft16 host NIC / ToR uplink) and
0.47 / 0.29 / 0.10 / 0.07 (ft128 host NIC / ToR uplink / agg uplink / core
downlink). Queues stay short: ToR uplinks peak at 51-54 packets on ft16 and
at a median of 19 (max 64) on ft128, against RoCE's thousands of packets in
host NICs. The window clamp keeps every source at or below line rate
(W <= W_init, so W / T <= B), and each ACK carries the current queue and
txRate of every hop, so a flow whose path fills up cuts within an RTT
instead of waiting for an ingress counter to pass 150 packets. That removes
RoCE's head-of-line coupling (point 4): in HPCC no flow is ever stopped
because a different flow's next hop is congested.

### 3. With overlapping requests on one host, HPCC is work conserving per flow

The NIC queue in front of every host is still per-flow round robin, but HPCC
flows do not fill it: each is paced at its own W / T, set by its own path's
U. A shard on a free path therefore takes the NIC capacity that a shard on a
collided path cannot use. RoCE does the opposite: every RoceSrc on a host
sends at line rate into the round-robin NIC queue and is stopped together
with the others when the ToR pauses the host (queue.cpp:461-466, 516-519).
Host pair 0 -> 8 at ft16 load 70, seed 1, shows it: request 3's shard starts
at 3.06 ms while three older shards are still on that pair. Under HPCC it
finishes at 8.3 ms (1.58 x floor); under RoCE at 36.0 ms (9.82 x floor),
within 0.4 ms of requests 1 and 2 (35.7 and 35.9 ms), because RoCE
moves all flows of a host at one shared pace.

That is why HPCC beats RoCE on ft16, where every request uses the same 8 host
pairs and overlap is the rule (79-748 same-sender overlapping request pairs
per load at 50-90% over 3 seeds, against 17-99 on ft128): p50 5.69 / 7.74 / 11.80 against 6.87 / 8.65 / 14.41 at
50 / 70 / 90%, x_ideal p50 2.71 / 3.24 / 3.69 against 3.06 / 3.80 / 4.28. The
low-load deficit of point 1 turns into a 10-18% gain once requests overlap.

### 4. Staggered arrivals: newcomers hold an outsized share

A new flow starts at W_init, i.e. at line rate. When it joins a link that
is already at eta, every flow's U rises and every flow cuts by the same
factor eta / U, which preserves the ratio between windows; only W_AI moves
the flows towards equal shares. HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md (HPCC validation a)
measured that this takes about 1.75 ms after a join at N = 10 (350 us at
N = 2), and validation d showed newer requests holding an outsized share
for milliseconds. KV shards here last 3-30 ms, so it matters.

The signature is a later request finishing before an earlier one that
shares a sender with it and overlaps it in time. Over 3 seeds:

| fabric, load | RoCE | HPCC | EQDS | DCQCN |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ft16 30 / 50 / 70 / 90 | 0/19, 0/86, 0/252, 0/748 | 1/18, 8/79, 12/213, 37/624 | 0/6, 2/54, 2/114, 9/211 | 2/27, 19/114, 31/312, 75/817 |
| ft128 30 / 50 / 70 / 90 | 0/2, 0/17, 0/52, 0/98 | 0/2, 1/20, 7/53, 25/99 | -, -, 0/5, 1/50 | 0/3, 0/21, 1/53, 14/99 |

(later-finishes-first / overlapping same-sender request pairs.) RoCE never
reorders requests: its NIC gives every flow of a host the same rate, so the
older one, having less left, always finishes first. HPCC reorders 5-10% of
pairs on ft16 and 13% and 25% on ft128 at 70 and 90%. The older request's
shards are the ones squeezed, which is the HPCC tail on ft128: p99 x_ideal
8.30 against RoCE's 6.71 at load 70 and 9.92 against 8.98 at 90, while the
medians tie (4.84 vs 4.81, 6.11 vs 6.17). On ft128 the benefit of point 3
is small because requests rarely share a sending host, and the 5% headroom
and the newcomer squeeze cancel it.

The N = 2 sensitivity run (ft16 load 70, W_AI = 16,375 B,
`results/e3/ft16_hpccN2/`) reduces reordering from 12/213 to 3/196 pairs
and improves p50 from 7.74 to 6.59 and p99 from 11.65 to 10.22, consistent
with HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md HPCC validation d (load 30: N = 2 beat N = 10 on
every seed). This is a real HPCC parameter (the paper's N is the expected
number of concurrent flows per link), not a bug; the tables report N = 10.

### 5. Jain and deadlines

Jain is 0.935-0.975 on ft16 and 0.933-0.953 on ft128, in RoCE's range, for
the same reason: every request carries at least one collided shard.
Deadline miss is 1.00 everywhere.

**Answer for HPCC:** INT-based rate control removes PFC pauses
(pause share <= 0.005 everywhere) and decouples the flows that share a host,
which makes HPCC 10-18% better than RoCE on ft16 once requests overlap. It
does not remove ECMP collisions, so at low load it is RoCE's collision
lottery at 95% of line rate (3.2-3.7 x floor at 30%), and on ft128 it ties
RoCE at the median with a worse tail at 70%, because newcomers start at line
rate and keep the larger share for ~1.75 ms at N = 10.

## Why (EQDS)

EQDS here is `build_eqds2` (stale-NACK, reorder-window, finished-flow-credit
and strict-priority fixes). Mechanisms, all confirmed in the runs below:

- **Spraying.** `-strat ecmp_host` sets the host route strategy `ECMP_FIB`
  and the switch strategy `FatTreeSwitch::ECMP` (main_eqds.cpp:331-333; with
  no `-strat` the default is also `ECMP_FIB`, main_eqds.cpp:383-384).
  `-paths 16` from run.sh sets 16 entropies (main_eqds.cpp:271, 469;
  binary default 64, main_eqds.cpp:47). Every packet gets the next entropy
  (`nextEntropy`, eqds.cpp:1238; `set_pathid` at eqds.cpp:1323, 1365, 1402)
  and every switch hashes `(flow_id, pathid)` per packet
  (fat_tree_switch.cpp:339). No flow is pinned to a link.
- **Receiver credit.** One pull pacer per receiving host at
  0.99 x linkspeed (main_eqds.cpp:550), serving its sinks round robin
  (eqds.cpp:2280-2293). A source sends a speculative 64-packet window, then
  only on credit (main_eqds.cpp:33, 573-574).
- **Trimming instead of PFC.** COMPOSITE queues (main_eqds.cpp:57): when the
  data queue is full, a payload is cut to a header that jumps the queue
  (compositequeue.cpp:183-211); the sink NACKs it and the retransmission is
  pulled first (`_rtx_senders`, eqds.cpp:2253).

### 1. Spraying removes the collision classes

At 30% load EQDS is at 1.28 x floor on ft16 and 1.26 on ft128 (p99 1.82 and
1.39), against 3.46 / 3.04 for RoCE and 3.72 / 3.18 for HPCC, with the ideal
at 1.00. On ft16 load 30 the 40 flows of the five requests that overlapped no
other request finished at 1.04-1.33 x floor on every seed; there are no 2x
or 3x classes. It
is the first policy with a deadline miss rate below 1: 0.00 at 30% on both
fabrics and 0.00 on ft128 up to 70%.

### 2. The residual 1.2-1.3x: uplink imbalance, trims, credit overhead

1.26 is not 1.03 (the single-flow EQDS number, E1 §3.2: 1.57% header and
a credit pacer that covers 0.98556 of C). The queue samplers
(`results/e3/ft128_1os/diag/eqds_ft128_1os_load30_s1_*`) show where the rest
goes: on ft128 at load 30, 28 of the 64 active ToR uplinks and 10 agg
uplinks reach the 2,000-packet trim point at some time (ToR uplink peak
occupancy median 882 packets), while no receiver ToR downlink does (peak
1,100) and core downlinks never queue. In the split placement a TP = 8
instance fills two ToRs, 4 sending hosts each, and every receiver pulls at
99 Gbps, so the 4 uplinks of a sending ToR carry 99% of their capacity while
its request is active. Each flow's 16 entropies hash unevenly over those
uplinks (E1 §6.2 measured 0.60-0.945 busy fractions for sprayed links), and
any uplink that draws more than a quarter is overloaded and fills until it
trims. The receivers cannot see it: credit is sized to their own downlink.
The trims come back as retransmissions, 120,030 [97,956-165,065] at load 30
(4.1% of the 2.96 M new packets), and each full uplink adds up to
2,000 x 4,150 B x 8 / 100 G = 664 us of queueing. On ft16 (load 90 sampled)
the picture is the same at 500 packets: all 8 active ToR uplinks and all 8
agg uplinks reach the trim point, receiver downlinks peak at 162-286
packets, core downlinks at 1. Retransmissions are 2.6-3.6% of new packets on
ft16 and 3.5-4.1% on ft128; stale NACKs, RTS and bounces are 0 in every run,
so no packet outlived its RTO.

### 3. ft16: overlap is processor sharing, which is what the ideal assumes

On ft16 x_floor rises from 1.28 to 3.93 with load but x_ideal stays at
1.25-1.28 at every load. All requests use the same 8 host pairs, so
overlapping requests share receivers; the receiver's pacer round-robins
its pulls over them (eqds.cpp:2280-2293), i.e. processor sharing, which is
how the fluid ideal shares a host too. EQDS's deadline miss equals the
ideal fabric's (0.00 / 0.60 / 0.89 at 30 / 50 / 90%, 0.81 vs 0.71 at 70).
Jain is lower than RoCE's at 50-90% (0.878-0.907) and matches the ideal
fabric's own Jain (0.855-0.888, computed from the same fluid model): requests
that arrive into an idle period finish near 1.3x and those inside a busy
period near 4-6x, as in a perfect network. RoCE's higher Jain (0.92-0.98) is
uniform slowness, not fairness. Requests are rarely reordered (2-9 of
54-211 overlapping same-sender pairs, table in "Why (HPCC)" point 4).

### 4. ft128 at 90%: the fabric, not the receiver, saturates

Up to 70% EQDS stays at 1.26-1.35 (x_ideal 1.26-1.34). At 90% it jumps to
1.72 p50, 2.41 p99, x_ideal 1.51, and deadline miss 0.19 [0.11-0.22] while
the ideal misses none. The load 90 samples
(`results/e3/ft128_1os/diag/eqds_ft128_1os_load90_s1_*`, finish times
identical to the sweep run) show the full queues spreading from the sending
side into the receiving pods: 35 ToR uplinks and 28 agg uplinks reach 2,000
packets, and now also 22 core downlinks and 17 agg downlinks, while the ToR
downlinks into the receivers still peak at 829 packets and never trim. So
the receiver pacers keep the last hop clean, as designed, but at 90% the
pod-level links into pods 4-7 run hot enough that sprayed traffic from
several requests overloads some of them; retransmissions are
307,123 [274,904-316,370], 3.5% of new packets.

The second effect is amplification. Being 1.3x slower than the ideal at
70% means each request occupies its decode hosts 30% longer, so more of
them overlap there and share a pacer. Counting request pairs that share a
receiving host and overlap in time (seed 1): 18 for EQDS against 7 in the
ideal fabric at 90% (4 vs 2 at 70%, 0 vs 0 at 50%). RoCE and HPCC have 31
and 33 at 90%. That queueing, not a new loss mechanism, is why the
excess over the ideal grows from 1.34 to 1.51.

**Answer for EQDS:** per-packet spraying over 16 entropies removes ECMP
collision classes, so EQDS is 1.21-1.35 x floor up to 70% on ft128 and
tracks the ideal fabric within 1.25-1.28x on ft16 at every load, with
deadline misses at the ideal's own rate. The remaining 25-35% is spray
imbalance on the sending ToR's uplinks (trimmed at 2,000 packets,
3.5-4.1% retransmissions) plus the 3% credit and header overhead. At 90% on
ft128 full queues spread into the receiving pods and requests start to pile
up at the decode hosts: 1.72 p50, 19% deadline misses.

## Why (DCQCN)

DCQCN here is the paper-faithful reaction point (HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md,
"DCQCN made faithful to the paper"). It uses RED marking
(HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md "DCQCN switch marking: probabilistic (RED) ECN"; this
section was HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES_ECN.md until commit 8738552 merged it) and the
minimum-rate clamp (HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md "DCQCN: minimum rate clamp", commit
4d3e1b2). The binary is `build`.

run.sh passes `-dcqcn 100 -ecn_kmin $((GBPS/8)) -ecn_kmax 400 -ecn_pmax
0.01`. Kmin is 1 us of queue (50 KB at 400 G, 12 KB at 100 G), Kmax is
400 KB and Pmax is 1% (`red_mark`, queue_lossless_output.cpp:44-60, applied
to data packets at :174). The mechanism, in code order:

- **Notification point.** Per flow, it sends at most one CNP per 50 us
  (dcqcn.cpp:20, 309-331).
- **Start.** The reaction point starts at line rate with alpha = 1
  (dcqcn.cpp:51-55).
- **On each CNP** it sets R_T = R_C, R_C = R_C (1 - alpha / 2) with a floor
  of R_AI, and alpha = (1 - g) alpha + g with g = 1/256 (dcqcn.cpp:26,
  82-91).
- **Every 55 us without a CNP** it runs fast recovery for 5 periods, then
  additive increase by R_AI = 0.1% of line rate. Hyper increase starts once
  both the timer count and the byte counter (B = 10 MB, in bytes) exceed
  F = 5 (dcqcn.cpp:27-33, 120-146).

PFC is still on underneath, with the same lossless queues and thresholds as
RoCE. The reference smoke run (`SEED=13 ./scripts/run.sh dcqcn default chk
workloads/kv_llama70b_4k_tp8.cm`) gives 15,171.1 us, the value
HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md records for RED and again for the clamp.

**The clamp changed most of these runs, slightly.** The sweep was first run
with the binary from before 4d3e1b2. Those runs are kept in
`results/e3/{ft16,ft128_1os}/dcqcn_preclamp/`, and ft16 load 70 gave
12.35 / 12.24 / 11.46 on seeds 1-3, exactly the "red 50/400/1 %" row of
the RED section's results (d). The clamp commit says every other run is
bit-identical. That holds only where no flow's R_C falls below R_AI. Here,
18 of 24 E3 runs changed, by up to 5% at the p50 (ft128 load 90 seed 2:
5.98 -> 6.28) and 14% at the p99 (ft128 load 70 seed 1: 9.13 -> 7.88).
ft16 load 70 is now 12.19 / 12.39 / 11.06. The tables report the
clamped binary.

CNP counts come from `DCQCN_TRACE=1` reruns of every DCQCN run (one line
per CNP and per rate increase; dcqcn.cpp:100, 151). All 72 traced runs of
E2, E3 and E6 reproduce their sweep runs exactly. On ft16 every request uses
the same 8 host pairs, so the trace cannot be split per flow there. On ft128
every line maps to exactly one flow.

### 1. Startup collapse: every arrival cuts every collided flow 3-5 times

ft128 load 30, seed 1 (72 flows, 2,165 CNPs; the clamp never acts in this
run):

| x_floor class (rounded) | flows | cut in first 300 us | CNPs / flow (median) | R_C after the first 300 us (median) | mean rate (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1x | 12 | 1 | 0 | 100 Gbps | 98.3 Gbps |
| 2x | 23 | 21 | 18 | 12.5 Gbps | 45.5 Gbps |
| 3x | 21 | 21 | 25 | 6.2 Gbps | 33.9 Gbps |
| 4x | 14 | 14 | 56 | 3.1 Gbps | 27.4 Gbps |

A request's 8 shards start at line rate. Wherever ECMP put two of them on
one link, the queue passes Kmax within tens of microseconds: the first CNP
comes a median of 46 us after the start. All flows through that queue are
marked, and the first CNPs of a request's cut flows span a median of 42 us,
inside one 50 us NP window. 87% of all marks are forced (q > Kmax): 12,940
against 1,938 probabilistic. A line-rate newcomer drives the queue through
the 12-400 KB RED band faster than one CNP window. The RED section of
HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md found the same at 400 G: "RED mostly does not act
during transients".

Alpha is still 1.000 at those cuts (median), so each CNP halves R_C. The NP
sends a CNP every 50 us while marked packets keep arriving. A two-way
collision therefore takes 3 halvings in the first 300 us (100 -> 12.5 Gbps),
a three-way 4 and a four-way 5. The fair shares would be 50, 33 and 25 Gbps.

On ft16 the arrivals hit flows that are already running. In the load 70
trace (seed 1), 3,146 of the 7,961 CNPs (40%) fall within 300 us after a
request arrival. Those windows cover 4.6% of the run. Every arrival after
the first cuts all 8 host pairs, down to the clamp (R_C = 0.4 Gbps).

### 2. Recovery at 0.1% of line rate per 55 us

A CNP sets R_T to the rate it cuts from (dcqcn.cpp:84). After back-to-back
cuts the target itself is therefore low. Fast recovery ((R_T + R_C) / 2 for
5 periods, dcqcn.cpp:126-128) only returns to the rate before the last cut.
Beyond that, additive increase adds R_AI = 100 Mbps per 55 us at 100 G
(400 Mbps at 400 G), i.e. 1.8 Gbps/ms (7.3 Gbps/ms). Hyper increase needs
60 MB sent since the last CNP (BC > F = 5 with B = 10 MB), more than a
third of a 167.8 MB shard.

Measured over each flow's longest CNP-free stretch, R_C grows at a median
of 2.65 Gbps/ms (63 flows; e.g. 32.4 -> 84.9 Gbps in 8.97 ms). The collided
flows therefore spend most of their first floor below their share. From the
startup cuts, they take a median of 11.3 ms (2x class), 10.0 ms (3x) and
10.8 ms (4x) to get back to 90% of the fair share, against a 13.4 ms floor.
The classes shift up by about one: summed over seeds 1-3 at load 30, DCQCN
has 43 / 72 / 65 / 36 flows at 1x / 2x / 3x / 4x and above, against RoCE's
57 / 95 / 54 / 10.

The recovery takes a fixed number of 55 us periods, because R_AI scales
with line rate. The floor does not: a shard lasts 3.36 ms at 400 G and
13.4 ms at 100 G. The same recovery therefore costs about four times as
many floors on ft16. That is why DCQCN / RoCE at load 30 is 1.43-1.84 per
seed on ft16 and 1.17-1.23 on ft128 (p50 5.54 against 3.46, and 3.54
against 3.04).

### 3. Staggered arrivals reorder requests

A new flow's RP starts at R_C = R_T = line rate (dcqcn.cpp:51-53). An
incumbent's R_T was captured at its last cut. So the newcomer is cut from a
higher rate and recovers towards a higher target (HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md,
DCQCN validation (a) point 3: newcomer at 152 Gbps against 47-61). Later
requests finish before earlier ones that share a sender with them:

| fabric | load 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ft16, DCQCN | 2/27 | 19/114 | 31/312 | 75/817 |
| ft128, DCQCN | 0/3 | 0/21 | 1/53 | 14/99 |

(later-finishes-first / overlapping same-sender request pairs, seeds 1-3.)
RoCE is 0 everywhere; HPCC and EQDS are in "Why (HPCC)" point 4. DCQCN
reorders 7-17% of the pairs on ft16, more than HPCC's 5-10%, because its
newcomer advantage is larger. An incumbent cut to a few Gbps needs about
10 ms to recover (point 2), against HPCC's 1.75 ms convergence after a join.

### 4. What DCQCN does remove: PFC pauses, and with them the RoCE tail at 90%

| seed 1, load 70 | host NIC | ToR uplink | agg uplink | core downlink | agg / ToR downlink |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DCQCN ft16 | 0.009 | 0.004 | 0.000 | 0.000 | 0.000 / 0.000 |
| DCQCN ft128 | 0.001 | 0.002 | 0.001 | 0.001 | 0.001 / 0.000 |
| RoCE ft16 | 0.34 | 0.12 | 0.00 | 0.00 | < 0.01 |
| RoCE ft128 | 0.47 | 0.29 | 0.10 | 0.07 | < 0.01 |

(Pause share while backlogged, from `results/e3/{ft16,ft128_1os}/diag/dcqcn_*`:
queue-sampler reruns with run.sh's DCQCN flags, with finish times identical
to the sweep runs.) Marks come before pauses. Kmax = 400 KB sits below the
600 KB per-ingress XOFF (150 packets of 4 KB, main_roce.cpp:358), so a
queue fed by one ingress is marked before it can pause it
(HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md, "PFC caveat"). The cuts then keep the ingress
counters low.

On ft128 at load 90, where RoCE's pauses spread across the fabric, this pays
off in the tail. DCQCN's worst request is better than RoCE's on every seed
(9.17 / 8.00 / 8.61 against 13.23 / 8.33 / 10.52 x floor). p99 is 8.37
against 10.40 and Jain 0.974 against 0.925, at a 3% higher p50 (6.36
against 6.17). The worst request is also better at load 50 (6.03 / 7.19 /
5.58 against 6.49 / 7.33 / 6.10). On ft16, where overlap is the rule, the
collapse at every arrival dominates. There DCQCN is worse than RoCE on
every seed at every load (p50 x 1.02-1.84).

### 5. Deep cuts reach the rate floor, which the clamp now holds at R_AI

Back-to-back CNPs stop only when the queue drains, so a flow whose queue
stays marked for a millisecond gets 20 cuts in a row (dcqcn.cpp:85). On ft128
many flows reach the floor. In the traced reruns, 0-1 flows per run hit
R_C = R_AI = 0.1 Gbps at 30%. At 50% it is 2-16, at 70% 8-16 and at 90%
16-24 (of 72-216). The longest any flow spends below 1 Gbps is 0.5-9.0 ms
per run. On ft16 (R_AI = 0.4 Gbps) the clamp acts in 8 of the 12 runs, on
1-8 of the 8 host pairs: 1-60 clamped cuts per run at 30-70% and 132-219
at 90%. The 4 runs where it never acts are exactly the 4 that are
bit-identical to the pre-clamp sweep.

Before the clamp there was no floor. RoceSrc books its next send when it
sends a packet, at `_time_last_sent + _packet_spacing` (roce.cpp:340-343),
with the spacing computed from the current rate (`update_spacing`,
roce.h:48). A rate increase only recomputes the spacing (dcqcn.cpp:148-149)
and does not move the booked send. Only a PFC RESUME (roce.cpp:229) wakes
the source early. So a flow cut to a few Mbps stayed silent for as long as
its last gap, even after its R_C had recovered.

The pre-clamp seed-1 run at 70% shows it. Flow 34 -> 114 was at 67.7 Gbps
at 53.5 ms, got one CNP every 50 us, and was at 1.9 Mbps at 54.9 ms: a
17 ms gap. Its R_C was back above 1 Gbps at 55.8 ms and above 10 Gbps at
60.7 ms, yet the queue sampler shows no delivery in 23% of its 771 50 us
samples (`results/e3/ft128_1os/diag/preclamp/`). With the clamp, the
longest gap is (4,000 + 64) B x 8 / 0.1 Gbps = 325 us at 100 G. In the
same run the most-stalled flow is silent in 2.3% of its samples (median 0),
and p99 drops from 9.13 to 7.88. Without the clamp, this mechanism stopped a
flow for good in one E2 run (docs/E2.md "Why (DCQCN)" point 4).

### 6. Jain and deadlines

Deadline miss is 1.00 everywhere. Jain is 0.963-0.984 on ft16 (RoCE
0.918-0.978) and 0.955-0.982 on ft128. Like RoCE, DCQCN is uniformly slow:
every request carries a collided, collapsed shard.

**Answer for DCQCN:** DCQCN is the slowest policy at the median at every
load on both fabrics (ft16 p50 5.54 / 7.83 / 12.19 / 18.39, ft128 3.54 /
4.51 / 5.40 / 6.36). The cause is how it reacts to the ECMP collisions it
cannot avoid. Every collided flow is cut 3-5 times in its first 300 us
(alpha = 1, one CNP per 50 us window, 87% of marks forced). It then climbs
back at 0.1% of line rate per 55 us, which takes about 10 ms: nearly one
100 G floor and three 400 G floors. DCQCN removes PFC pauses (pause share
<= 0.009). That gives it a better tail than RoCE on ft128 at 50% and 90%,
but not a better median.

The first-order effect in E3 is load balancing, not congestion control: the
three pinned-path transports (RoCE, HPCC, DCQCN) are 2.3-4.7x worse than the
sprayed one at every load and on both fabrics. Relative to RoCE, congestion
control moves the median by -18% to +8% (HPCC) and by +3% to +60% (DCQCN).
DCQCN makes the pinned-path result worse: it reacts to collisions it cannot
remove by cutting the collided flows far below their fair share. Its one
gain, removing PFC, shows only in the ft128 tail at 50% and 90%.
