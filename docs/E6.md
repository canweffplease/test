# E6: communication pattern (incast)

**Question.** How does each mechanism handle KV shards from K prefill instances
converging on one decode GPU, compared with the non-incast TP=8 permutation?

**Status.** RoCE (PFC only, no CC), HPCC, EQDS and DCQCN done, 3 seeds
each, every run complete and with zero lossless violations.

## Why (RoCE)

### 1. Under incast the receiver downlink is the only bottleneck, and PFC keeps it 100% busy

The slowest shard finishes at 1.016-1.018 x (K x floor) for every K and seed.
The pfc_diag runs (`results/e6/diag/roce_default_incast{4,8}_s{1,2}_pfc.md`)
show the ToR -> host 8 queue (`LS4->DST8`) at utilization 0.999-1.000 over
the whole transfer and never paused. A link that never idles while there are
bytes to deliver is the definition of meeting the bound.

The remaining 1.6% is packet headers, not waste: a RoCE data packet carries
`Packet::data_packet_size()` = 4,000 payload bytes (roce.cpp:36) and is put on
the wire as `size + ACKSIZE` with `ACKSIZE = 64` (rocepacket.h:46, 68), and
4,064 / 4,000 = 1.016. parse.py's floor counts payload only.

Nothing is lost on the way: PFC makes every switch hop lossless
(LosslessInputQueue sends PAUSE when an ingress port's accounted bytes pass
the high threshold and RESUME below the low one,
queue_lossless_input.cpp:70-72, 100-102; thresholds are 150 / 80 packets,
main_roce.cpp:345), and the htsim_roce `Rtx:` counter, which counts
go-back-N resends after a NACK (roce.cpp:146), is 0 in all 12 runs.

### 2. The pauses travel three hops upstream

The same diagnostics show where the backpressure goes. For K = 8, seed 1,
the pause share (paused / (paused + served) while the queue had packets) is
0.43 on the agg -> ToR downlinks feeding host 8's ToR, 0.62 on the
core -> agg downlinks, 0.80 on the agg -> core uplinks and 0.82 on the
ToR -> agg uplinks of the senders, 7 ToR uplinks and 6 agg uplinks paused for
more than 1% of the run (up to 20.4 ms each, 224 pause episodes). Each
paused queue is paused because the next switch's per-ingress counter
(LosslessInputQueue::_queuesize, decremented only when the packet leaves
that switch's egress queue, queue_lossless_output.cpp:152) is full of packets
waiting for the congested downlink. A paused LosslessOutputQueue stops all
its traffic (queue_lossless_output.cpp:50-57, there is a single traffic
class), so in a shared fabric any other flow crossing those 18 links would
be stalled too. In this matrix nothing else is running, so the pauses cost
the incast nothing; E2's victim test is where they do.

Host NICs are not paused in a way the diagnostic can see (pause share
< 0.01): with one flow per host, the NIC's FairPriorityQueue forwards the
PAUSE to its RoceSrc (queue.cpp:461-466), the source stops
(roce.cpp:321), and the NIC queue holds no backlog, which the lower-bound
pause detector needs (scripts/pfc_analyze.py docstring).

### 3. Shares are per ingress port, not per flow, so flows finish staggered

RoCE does not divide the downlink equally among the K flows. In every K = 4
run one flow finishes at 2.04 x floor, i.e. it had 200 G, half of the 400 G
downlink, from start to finish, while the other three shared the other half.
The sink samplers in the diagnostic give the per-flow mean rates directly:
K = 4 seed 1: 197, 131, 98, 98 Gbps; K = 8 seed 2: two flows at 98 Gbps, six
at 49-50 Gbps.

That is the per-port fairness of PFC on a FIFO egress. At a congested egress
every backlogged ingress port cycles through the same 150 / 80 packet
pause / resume hysteresis and sends at line rate when resumed, so each
ingress PORT gets an equal share of the egress, however many flows it
carries. Host 8's ToR has two ingress ports from its two aggs: the diagnostic
for K = 4 seed 1 shows `US5->LS_4` carrying one flow and `US4->LS_4` three, and
the one flow got 200 G. Splitting again by port at the agg (core ingress
ports) and at the source side reproduces the measured finish times exactly:
for K = 4 seed 1, 1/2 for flow 3 (done at 6,831 us), then 1/4 and 1/8 for the
others until flow 3 leaves, and so on, giving 6,831 / 10,240 / 13,649 /
13,649 us. Which flow is lucky depends only on the ECMP hash (seed), so the
order changes across seeds while the last-finish time does not.

So the finish order is staggered in quantized steps (multiples of the floor),
not processor sharing: the first flow lands at 0.51 of the incast bound, the
last at 1.016, finish spread 0.50, Jain 0.94-0.96. Total throughput is
perfect; per-request fairness is not. For KV caches this matters when the K
flows are shards of different requests: some decode instances get their
cache in half the time of others, for reasons unrelated to size or arrival.

### 4. Without incast, RoCE is worse

The TP=8 reference has no shared endpoint, yet its slowest shard is 2.04-3.05 x
floor, worse than any incast relative to its own bound. Each RoCE flow
pins one path (`_pathid = random()%256` once per source, roce.cpp:66, stamped
on every packet, roce.cpp:294; the switch hashes it,
fat_tree_switch.cpp:339), and on this tree a cross-pod flow has only 4 paths
(HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md, "What the spread physically is"). Two or three
flows hashed onto one 400 G link each get 1/2 or 1/3 of it for the whole
transfer, so per-flow results are exactly 1.02 / 2.03 / 3.05 x floor
(1.02 = header overhead). Under incast the bottleneck is the receiver link,
which no path choice avoids, so the path lottery stops mattering: ECMP
collisions upstream only shift WHICH flow gets which share, never the
last-finish time.

**Answer for RoCE:** the receiver downlink bound dominates and RoCE converges
onto it (1.016 x K x floor, of which 1.6% is headers). It wastes no
bandwidth under incast. Its costs are elsewhere: pauses spread to every
upstream link the incast touches, and the K flows get unequal,
topology-dependent shares, so they finish staggered rather than together.

## Why (HPCC)

HPCC here is the paper-faithful sender (`build_hpccfix`): per ACK
`W = Wc / (U / eta) + W_AI`, Wc updated once per RTT, W clamped to
[MSS, W_init] (`computeWindPaper`, hpcc.cpp:583-601), pacing at W / T
(`setWindow`, hpcc.cpp:603-610), eta = 0.95 (hpcc.cpp:38),
W_init = B x T = 655,000 B at 400 G and T = 13.1 us (hpcc.cpp:91, 107), and
W_AI = W_init (1 - eta) / N = 3,275 B with N = 10 (hpcc.cpp:40, 116). U comes
from INT that every switch egress stamps on data packets (qlen, timestamp,
tx bytes, link rate; queue_lossless_output.cpp:186-201).

### 1. HPCC meets the incast bound minus its designed 5% headroom

The slowest shard is at 1.066 (K = 2), 1.059 (K = 4) and 1.047 (K = 8) x
(K x floor), the same in all three seeds. A link held at eta = 0.95 with
9,000 B payload in 9,064 B frames gives 1 / (0.95 x 0.993) = 1.060, which is
K = 2 and K = 4 exactly. K = 8 is better than eta because 8 flows each add
W_AI = 3,275 B per RTT, 26 KB in total, 4% of B x T, so U sits slightly above
eta and the link runs at 96.2% of wire rate instead of 95%. The fidelity test
saw the same 1.046 on `kv_incast_8to1.cm` (HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md, HPCC
validation c). RoCE runs the downlink at 100% of the wire, so on this metric
HPCC is 3-5% behind RoCE: the price of the headroom, paid on every byte.

What the headroom buys is visible in the queues. For K = 8, seed 1, the
receiver downlink `LS4->DST8` peaks at 40 packets (363 KB) and is above the
15-packet PFC threshold in 0.9% of samples; RoCE's same queue peaks at 325
packets and sits above its 150-packet threshold 69% of the time
(`results/e6/diag/roce_default_incast8_s1_queues.csv`). HPCC's pause share is
0.016-0.024 on the ToR uplinks, agg uplinks and core downlinks the incast
crosses (0.4-0.7 ms of pause per class in total), against RoCE's 0.80-0.82 three hops upstream. For K = 2 the downlink
queue never exceeds one packet and nothing is paused. HPCC sees the receiver
link's txRate and queue through INT and stops the senders before PFC has to.

### 2. K >= 4 is close to processor sharing; K = 2 is not

For K = 4 and 8 the K flows finish within 2-6% of each other (spread 0.02-0.06,
Jain 1.000), the 2.4-3.2% first-to-last spread of the fidelity test. All K
flows start together at W_init, every flow's U is the same downlink's U, and
the MIMD step `Wc / (U / eta)` cuts all windows by the same factor, so they
stay near-equal. The flows still finish in a different order on every seed
(the first at 0.995-1.022 of the bound, the last at 1.047-1.059), which is the
residual per-flow bias in U (below).

K = 2 is unfair: one flow finishes at 1.67 x floor and the other at 2.13 on
seeds 1 and 2 (1.79 / 2.13 on seed 3), a rate split of about 1.7:1 while both
are active. The multiplicative step preserves whatever ratio the windows
have, and only W_AI equalizes them. HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md ("What still differs
from an ideal fluid HPCC") measured why the ratio is not 1: two flows on one
bottleneck see U differing by ~0.006, because each sees the queue at its own
packets' dequeue instants and one 9 KB packet is 1.4% of B x T. With N = 10,
U sits only ~1% above eta, so `1 - eta / U` is ~0.01 and a 0.006 bias splits
the windows strongly. At K = 2 there is also too little aggregate additive
increase (2 x 3,275 B, 1% of B x T) to hold U above eta. The sensitivity run
confirms the mechanism: with `-hpcc_N 2` (W_AI = 16,375 B) the two flows finish
at 2.05 / 2.06 x floor, within 0.5%, and the pair finishes 3.6% sooner
(6,897 us, 1.03 x bound, instead of 7,154 us), because the larger additive term holds U
further above eta. The run.sh default stays N = 10 (HTSIM's constant, the
paper's rule N = expected concurrent flows per link); N = 2 is a sensitivity
point, not what the table reports.

For KV transfer, the HPCC K = 2 result means two decode-bound shards of
different requests can get 60/40 of the receiver for the whole transfer,
less skewed than RoCE's port-level split (one flow at 0.51 of the bound for
K >= 4) but not processor sharing.

### 3. Without incast, HPCC is RoCE's collision lottery plus 5%

The TP = 8 reference lands in RoCE's collision classes scaled by 1/eta:
1.049 (alone on its path), 2.13-2.19 (two flows on a link, or a parking lot,
OUR_CHANGES.md HPCC validation b), 3.07-3.19 (three-way). HPCC, like RoCE,
draws one path id per flow for its whole life (`_pathid = random() % 256`,
hpcc.cpp:81, stamped on every packet at hpcc.cpp:390, hashed per switch at
fat_tree_switch.cpp:339), so rate control cannot move a shard off a collided
link: it can only share that link fairly at 95%. Seed 1 (2.19) and seeds 2 / 3
(3.19) are the same placements as RoCE's 2.04 / 3.05.

**Answer for HPCC:** under incast HPCC holds the receiver downlink at its
target, 94-96% of the wire (1.047-1.066 x bound), with a queue of at most 40
packets and almost no PFC. It shares close to equally for K >= 4 but splits
two flows 1.7:1 at N = 10 (equal at N = 2). Without incast it inherits the
ECMP collision classes, 5% slower than RoCE in each.

## Why (EQDS)

EQDS here is `build_eqds2`. Each receiving host has one pull pacer
(`pacers[dest]`, main_eqds.cpp:550, rate 0.99 x linkspeed), and each pull
grants `_credit_per_pull` = 8 x 512 B of credit (eqds.cpp:22-23). Every
source sends a speculative first window of `cwnd` = 64 packets without
credit (DEFAULT_CWND, main_eqds.cpp:33; `setCwnd` / `setMaxWnd`,
main_eqds.cpp:573-574), then only on credit. Packets are sprayed over 16
entropies (`-paths 16` from run.sh, main_eqds.cpp:271, 469; `nextEntropy`,
eqds.cpp:1238, `set_pathid` at eqds.cpp:1323 / 1365 / 1402) on COMPOSITE
(trimming) queues (main_eqds.cpp:57).

### 1. Exact processor sharing at the receiver

For K = 4 and 8 all K flows finish within 1 us of each other (4.123 x floor
for all 4, 8.239 for all 8, on every seed; spread 0.000, Jain 1.000). The
pacer serves `_active_senders` round robin: `doNextEvent` takes
`mostUrgent(_active_senders)`, which with equal priorities is the list front
(`EqdsPullPacer::mostUrgent`, eqds.cpp:2376-2384, keeps the first of the lowest priority), issues one pull,
and pushes the sink to the back if it still has demand (eqds.cpp:2280-2293).
With no priorities set, `preemptingSink` never preempts
(HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md, "No-prio behaviour is identical"). Every flow gets
every K-th pull, so every flow gets 1/K of the receiver link from the first
RTT to the last packet, which is the definition of processor sharing. Unlike
RoCE (first flow at 0.51 of the bound, set by which ingress port the hash
used) and HPCC (set by U biases), the order and rates do not depend on the
path the packets took.

K = 2 finishes 2% apart (2.02-2.03 vs 2.07, spread 0.02). Its last flow
still finishes at 1.033 x bound. I did not trace the 2% (about 135 us);
the faster flow is host 0 on seed 1 and host 1 on seeds 2 and 3.

### 2. The 3% to the bound is credit pacing and headers, not congestion

The slowest shard is at 1.030-1.033 x bound for every K and seed. E1 §3.2
computes 1.0306 for a single EQDS flow on an idle path: 64 B of header per
4,086 B payload (1.57%) and a pacer that grants 4,096 B per pull at
one pull every 0.99 x 8 x `pktByteTimes(_mtu)` / C, with
`pktByteTimes` = 4,160 + 38 B (eqds.cpp:2192-2195, interval at eqds.cpp:2219) while the source spends 4,150 B of credit per packet, so steady credit
covers 0.98556 of C. The incast reproduces that number exactly, which says
the receiver link is driven by the pacer and nothing else: wire utilization
0.983-0.986. RoCE is 1.4% better here (it fills the wire), HPCC 2.3-4% worse.

### 3. No trims under incast

The trimming EQDS was expected to pay at t = 0 did not happen: `Rtx` is 0 in
all nine incast runs (`log_counters.rtx_pkts`, 0 stale NACKs). For K = 8 the
speculative windows put 8 x 64 = 512 packets in flight at once, slightly
more than the 500-packet egress, but they reach host 8's ToR over two 400 G
agg links while the 400 G downlink drains, so the queue peaks well below
500 (`CompositeQueue::receivePacket` trims only when `_queuesize_low` would
pass `_maxsize`, compositequeue.cpp:183-211). After the first RTT the only
data in flight is what the pacer credited, at 98.6% of the downlink. The
receiver-driven design simply never offers the downlink more than it can
take.

### 4. The TP = 8 reference: spraying, but over a finite entropy set

Without incast EQDS is 1.28-1.32 x floor (seed 13 gave 1.24 in E1, §6), far
better than RoCE's 2.04-3.05 and HPCC's 2.19-3.19, because no shard is pinned
to a collided link. It is not 1.03 because 16 entropies hash unevenly onto
the 2 ToR uplinks and 2 agg uplinks of each path (E1 §6.2: the most loaded
core link runs at 0.945 busy while others sit at 0.60), and the overloaded
uplinks trim: 6,230-11,313 retransmissions per run, 1.9-3.4% of the 328,488
packets. The spread across shards (0.14-0.21) is that imbalance; it is
roughly a third of RoCE's 0.50-0.67.

**Answer for EQDS:** under incast EQDS is exact processor sharing at
1.030 x bound, every K flows finishing together, with no trims and a
downlink at 98.6% of the wire. The remaining 3% is header and credit-pacing
overhead that E1 already measured for a single flow. Without incast, spraying
removes RoCE's and HPCC's collision classes (1.28-1.32 x floor); the residual
is the hash imbalance of 16 entropies.

## Why (DCQCN)

DCQCN is the paper-faithful reaction point with RED marking and the
minimum-rate clamp, binary `build` at commit 4d3e1b2. It runs with run.sh's
flags `-dcqcn 100 -ecn_kmin 50 -ecn_kmax 400 -ecn_pmax 0.01
-pfc_thresholds 80 150`. The mechanism and its file:line references are in
docs/E3.md "Why (DCQCN)" (HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES.md "DCQCN made faithful to the
paper" and "DCQCN switch marking: probabilistic (RED) ECN"; the latter was
HTSIM/OUR_CHANGES_ECN.md until commit 8738552 merged it). In short:

- A sink sends at most one CNP per 50 us (dcqcn.cpp:20, 309-331).
- A flow starts at R_C = R_T = line rate with alpha = 1 (dcqcn.cpp:51-55).
- Each CNP sets R_T = R_C and R_C = R_C (1 - alpha / 2) (dcqcn.cpp:84-85),
  with R_C held at R_AI or above (dcqcn.cpp:90).
- Recovery runs in 55 us periods (dcqcn.cpp:120-146), with
  R_AI = 0.1% of line rate.

The tables use the clamp build. The runs from before the clamp are in
`results/e6/dcqcn_preclamp/`. They reproduced the RED section's smoke (b)
and incast (c) seeds 1-3 exactly: tp8 11,557 / 14,628 / 14,550 us and
incast8 32,560 / 33,693 / 32,182 us. The clamp changes only incast8 seed 1
(32,560 -> 32,805 us). That is the one run where 10 halvings from 400 G
reach 400 x 2^-10 = 0.39 Gbps, below R_AI = 0.4 Gbps.

Traces are seed-1 reruns with `DCQCN_TRACE=1` (one line per CNP and per rate
increase, dcqcn.cpp:100, 151) and `ECN_TRACE=1` (one line per mark,
queue_lossless_output.cpp:177-179). Their finish times are identical to the
table.

### 1. All K flows are cut together, and harder the larger K is

| K | fair share | forced marks (q > Kmax), all before | CNPs per flow, first ms | R_C after them | time to 90% of the share | CNPs per flow after 1 ms | mean R_C over the flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 200 G | 1,036, by 93 us | 3 | 50 G | 5.3 ms | 3 | 140 G |
| 4 | 100 G | 5,467, by 212 us | 5-6 | 6.25-12.5 G | 9.1-16.0 ms | 7-16 | 65-80 G |
| 8 | 50 G | 17,078, by 447 us | 10 | 0.40 G (the clamp) | 6.85 ms | 34-45 | 42-50 G |

All K shards start at line rate at t = 0 into host 8's one 400 G downlink.
The queue there passes Kmax = 400 KB within microseconds, every data packet
is marked, and every flow's sink returns its first CNP at 18-22 us. With
alpha = 1, each CNP halves R_C. The queue stays above Kmax until the K
flows' aggregate has dropped below 400 G and the backlog has drained. The
overshoot is K:1, so the backlog grows with K and so does the number of
50 us windows in which every flow is cut again: 3 at K = 2, 5-6 at K = 4
and 10 at K = 8. The aggregate falls to 100 G, about 30 G and 3.2 G,
which is 25%, 8% and 0.8% of the link. These startup marks are all forced
(q > Kmax). After 0.45 ms no packet sees the queue above Kmax again, and
the remaining marks (14 / 49 / 326 at K = 2 / 4 / 8) are probabilistic.

### 2. Recovery costs about the same time whatever K is, so the bound decides the penalty

From the cut, R_C climbs back by fast recovery towards R_T, the rate
before the last cut (dcqcn.cpp:126-128). Then additive increase adds
R_AI = 0.4 Gbps per 55 us (7.3 Gbps/ms, dcqcn.cpp:136-141). Hyper increase
starts once 60 MB have been sent since the last CNP (dcqcn.cpp:130-134).
The time to reach 90% of the fair share is 5.3 ms (K = 2), 9.1-16.0 ms
(K = 4) and 6.85 ms (K = 8). The incast bounds are 6.7, 13.4 and 26.8 ms.

- **K = 2.** Each flow spends most of its life below its share. Its mean
  R_C of 140 G is 70% of 200 G, and the slowest flow ends at
  1 / 0.70 = 1.46 x bound.
- **K = 4.** The recovery is as long as the bound: 1.50.
- **K = 8.** The recovery is a quarter of the bound. After it, the eight
  flows sit around their 50 G (R_C 21-88 G after 8 ms). Each additive step
  adds only 8 x 0.4 G per 55 us, so the queue rises slowly into the RED
  band, and RED cuts one flow at a time (RED section (c)). The receiver
  downlink is busy 83.1% of the run (`results/e6/diag/dcqcn_default_incast8_s1_*`,
  utilization 0.831). The slowest shard ends at 1.22 x bound.

That gentler overshoot after the startup is why incast is DCQCN's best
pattern relative to its bound. A large incast amortises one startup
collapse over a long transfer, and the small K = 2 and 4 incasts cannot.
Even so, DCQCN is the slowest of the four policies at every K: 1.46 /
1.50 / 1.22 x bound, against RoCE's 1.02, HPCC's 1.05-1.07 and EQDS's 1.03.

PFC barely acts. At K = 8 the receiver downlink peaks at 325 packets
(the same peak as RoCE). But it is above the 150-packet XOFF level in only
0.85% of samples, against RoCE's 69%, and it is never paused. Upstream pause
shares are 0.011-0.053 (RoCE 0.43-0.82), all in the startup.

### 3. Shares are per flow and fair, but not synchronized

Unlike RoCE (per ingress port under PFC), each DCQCN flow reacts to its own
CNPs, so no flow gets twice the share of another: Jain 0.994-1.000. For
K = 4 and 8 the flows finish 7-18% apart (spread 0.07-0.19), because RED
cuts them one at a time after the startup (the RED section's "individual
finishes spread"). At K = 2 both flows are cut in the same windows and
finish together (spread 0.00, except seed 3 at 0.11). The first flow
finishes at 1.03 x bound at K = 8, and nobody gets RoCE's "half the
requests in half the time".

### 4. Without incast, DCQCN is RoCE's collision lottery plus the collapse

DCQCN shares RoCE's path choice, the same `random() % 256` path id per flow
(roce.cpp:66) from the same seed. So the same flows collide in tp8, and
each collided flow then pays docs/E3.md point 1's startup collapse and
point 2's slow recovery. On seed 1 the five uncollided flows finish at
1.02 under both policies. The three collided ones finish at 2.27 / 2.59 /
3.44 x floor, against RoCE's 2.03 / 2.04 / 2.04. The slowest shard is
4.34 x floor (median), against RoCE's 3.05 and HPCC's 3.19, and Jain is
0.839.

**Answer for DCQCN:** at every K, DCQCN misses the incast bound by more
than the other three policies: 1.46 (K = 2), 1.50 (K = 4) and 1.22 (K = 8).
All K flows are cut 3-10 times in the first half millisecond, to 25%, 8%
and 0.8% of the link. The recovery at 0.1% of line rate per 55 us then
takes 5-16 ms to reach the fair share. That costs most at small K, whose
transfer is short. After the startup, RED keeps the queue below Kmax and
the shares fair (Jain >= 0.994), and PFC barely acts (downlink above XOFF
in 0.85% of samples).

For the slowest shard RoCE is best by 1.4-3%, because it alone fills the
wire. DCQCN is last at every K, by 20-48%, because it cuts all K flows
far below their share at the start and climbs back at 0.1% of line rate
per 55 us. For the order in which K requests' shards land, EQDS is the only
one that is fair by construction. RoCE gives half of the requests their
shard in half the time. HPCC is fair only with enough flows (or a larger
W_AI). DCQCN is fair per flow but spreads the finishes by up to 19%.
